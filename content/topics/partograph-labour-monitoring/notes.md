## 1. Purpose and Indications

The **partograph** (partogram) is a graphic record of labour on one page. It shows, at a glance, whether the **mother** is well, whether the **baby** is well and whether **labour is progressing**. It was designed by Philpott in Zimbabwe in the 1970s and promoted by WHO for low-resource settings, because it lets one midwife watching many women spot trouble early and decide to **refer or act** in time.

The **WHO Labour Care Guide (LCG)**, launched in 2020, is the "next-generation partograph". It keeps the idea but updates the science (active labour from 5 cm, no rigid 1 cm/hour rule), adds **supportive care** (companion, pain relief, fluids, posture) and **shared decision-making**, and uses **alert thresholds** for each observation instead of alert and action lines.

Why it matters in Uganda: obstructed labour (leading to ruptured uterus and fistula) and intrapartum stillbirth are still common, and both are largely prevented by **monitoring labour properly and acting on what the chart shows**. A partograph that is filled in after the delivery ("retrospective partograph") helps nobody.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: the partograph is a **decision tool, not a form**. Every time you plot a value, ask "is this normal?", and if it is not, write what you are doing about it in the plan.

**Indications**

- **Use for every woman in labour** in the facility (WHO), including those being augmented or induced and those on a trial of labour after caesarean.
- Start it in the **active phase only** (4 cm on the modified WHO partograph; 5 cm on the LCG).
- It is also the **referral document**: the chart goes with the woman, and the receiving facility continues it rather than starting again.
- It is a **legal record** and a key source for **maternal and perinatal death surveillance and response (MPDSR)** reviews.

> [!UGANDA]
> After the **Essential Maternal and Newborn Clinical Care Guidelines (2022)**, the Ministry of Health began replacing the partograph with the **LCG** and training providers (the rollout has been gradual, with Uganda-specific modifications studied in Mbarara). Mengo and other hospitals may use either tool during the transition. The Uganda Clinical Guidelines 2023 still describe partograph use. Learn to plot and interpret **both**, and ask your ward which one is the standard.

## 2. Contraindications and Precautions

- **Do not use the partograph as the main tool** (or be ready to deviate immediately) when there is an indication for urgent delivery: **APH, cord prolapse, eclampsia, fetal distress, malpresentation needing CS, preterm labour below 34 weeks** (special care), or **planned caesarean**.
- **Do not start in the latent phase.** Starting in the latent phase makes normal women appear "slow" and leads to unnecessary interventions (ARM, oxytocin, caesarean).
- **Slow dilatation alone** is not a reason for oxytocin or caesarean if mother and baby are well (WHO 2018); look for the cause first.
- **CTG is not a substitute**: WHO 2018 does **not recommend admission CTG or continuous CTG for low-risk women**, because it increases caesarean and instrumental deliveries without improving newborn outcomes.
- **Oxytocin augmentation** is only for facilities with caesarean capability and one-to-one monitoring; it is contraindicated in obstruction, malpresentation, fetal distress and previous classical CS (section 8).
- **ARM** is avoided when contraindicated, for example HIV with a detectable viral load.
- A chart that is **not filled at the time** is worse than useless: it gives false reassurance. Never fill a partograph retrospectively.

## 3. Relevant Anatomy and Normal Values

### Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Partograph** | Graphic record of labour progress plus fetal and maternal condition |
| **Composite WHO partograph (1994)** | Had a latent phase (0-3 cm, up to 8 h) and active phase from 3 cm |
| **Modified WHO partograph (2000)** | Latent phase removed; **active phase starts at 4 cm**. Used in many Ugandan facilities and in training |
| **Simplified partograph** | Colour-coded (green/amber/red zones) versions for lower-level facilities |
| **WHO Labour Care Guide (2020)** | Starts at **5 cm**; alert thresholds for every parameter; includes supportive care and shared decision-making |
| **Alert line** | Line from 4 cm at the time of first active-phase plot, rising **1 cm per hour** to 10 cm |
| **Action line** | Parallel line **4 hours to the right** of the alert line |
| **Intermittent auscultation (IA)** | Listening to the FHR with a Pinard stethoscope or hand-held Doppler at set intervals |
| **CTG (cardiotocography)** | Continuous electronic FHR and contraction recording; not recommended for low-risk women on admission or continuously (WHO 2018) |

### The physiology behind the alert line

Friedman's studies (1950s) showed that once the active phase starts, the cervix of most women dilates at **at least 1 cm per hour**. Philpott used this to draw the **alert line**: a woman whose dilatation stays on or to the left of the line is progressing normally. Falling to the right means she is slower than expected, which may reflect **inadequate contractions (Powers), malposition or a large baby (Passenger), or a small pelvis (Passage)**. The **4-hour gap** before the action line gives time to refer from a health centre to a hospital (the average transfer time Philpott observed), and to try simple measures.

Modern data (Zhang and the WHO BOLD study) show many normal women dilate **more slowly than 1 cm/hour, especially before 5-6 cm**. That is why the LCG removed the fixed alert line and uses **time-at-each-centimetre thresholds** instead, and why WHO says slow dilatation **alone** is not a reason for oxytocin or caesarean if mother and baby are well.

### Why each fetal parameter matters

- **FHR**: the fetus depends on placental exchange, which pauses during every contraction (the myometrium compresses the spiral arteries). A healthy fetus tolerates this; a compromised one shows **tachycardia, bradycardia or late decelerations** (the heart rate dips after the peak of a contraction and recovers late, because the fetus is becoming hypoxic during the contraction and recovering slowly).
- **Liquor**: **meconium** (especially thick) may indicate fetal hypoxia (hypoxia stimulates gut peristalsis and anal sphincter relaxation) and risks **meconium aspiration**. **Blood-stained liquor** can mean abruption or vasa praevia. **Absent liquor** after rupture means oligohydramnios, with higher risk of cord compression.
- **Moulding**: increasing moulding as labour goes on, especially +++, means the head is being squeezed through a pelvis that is too small (**CPD**).

### Why the maternal parameters matter

- **Pulse** rises early in **haemorrhage, sepsis, dehydration and exhaustion**. It is the cheapest early-warning sign.
- **BP** detects pre-eclampsia (can appear first in labour) and shock.
- **Temperature** detects **chorioamnionitis** and malaria.
- **Urine**: **ketones** = dehydration/starvation (a ketotic uterus contracts poorly); **protein** = pre-eclampsia (or contamination); **low volume** = dehydration or obstruction; **blood** = bladder compression in obstructed labour.

### Normal values at a glance

| Parameter | Normal |
|---|---|
| FHR baseline | **110-160 bpm**, regular, no late decelerations |
| Contractions (adequate, active phase) | **3-5 in 10 min**, each lasting **more than 40 s** |
| Cervical dilatation (modified partograph) | On or left of the alert line (**at least 1 cm/h** from 4 cm) |
| Descent | Head progressively descending in **fifths palpable** (5/5 fully above the brim, 0/5 fully in the pelvis) |
| Liquor | **I** (intact) or **C** (clear) |
| Moulding | **0** or **+** |
| Maternal pulse | Below 100 (LCG alert below 60 or 120 or more) |
| BP | Below 140/90 |
| Temperature | Below 37.5 degrees C |
| Urine | Passing regularly; no protein or acetone |

## 4. Equipment and Preparation

### Equipment

- Partograph or LCG form (one per woman), pen, watch with a second hand
- **Pinard fetal stethoscope** and/or **hand-held Doppler** (with gel, charged batteries)
- BP machine, thermometer, urine dipsticks and a measuring jug
- Sterile gloves, antiseptic, sterile pads for VEs
- A clock/timer and good light
- HemoCue (if anaemia or bleeding is suspected) and, where available, bedside ultrasound and CTG

### Consent, privacy and companion

- Introduce yourself, explain each observation and **ask permission before every abdominal examination and VE**. A student must be supervised and the woman may decline a student VE.
- Close curtains; keep the woman covered except for the area examined. Offer a **female chaperone** for VEs.
- Encourage a **companion of choice**, oral fluids and mobility: these are recorded on the LCG (supportive care) and an "N" is itself an alert.

### Position and exposure

- Abdominal palpation and FHR: woman semi-recumbent or in **left lateral tilt**; avoid prolonged **supine** lying (aortocaval compression; "SP" is an LCG alert).
- VE: dorsal position with knees flexed and apart, perineum exposed only for the examination.

### Focused history: what the chart needs, and why

Each item changes how you plot or interpret.

| Question | Why it matters to monitoring |
|---|---|
| When did regular contractions start? | Distinguishes latent vs active labour; tells you whether and where to start plotting |
| When did membranes rupture, and what colour was the fluid? | Write the time at the top of the chart. Over 18 h = infection risk (temperature more often, antibiotics per protocol); meconium = closer FHR monitoring |
| Parity and previous labours | Multiparas progress faster; a **slow multipara is obstruction until proved otherwise** |
| Previous caesarean | Trial of labour needs close monitoring for scar rupture (maternal pulse, scar pain, bleeding, FHR loss); any oxytocin needs senior approval |
| Hypertension, fever, diabetes, HIV | Adds items to monitor (BP, urine protein, temperature, glucose); affects ARM decision |
| Drugs already given (misoprostol for induction, oxytocin, pethidine, MgSO₄, antibiotics) and **when** | Tachysystole risk; neonatal respiratory depression after recent pethidine |
| Urge to push, bleeding, headache, fever since the last review | May bring the next VE forward |
| **Referral partograph** from a lower facility | Transfer the times and findings; do not start again "fresh" |

## 5. Procedure Step by Step

Do it in the same order every time: **look at the woman, look at the chart, examine, plot, interpret, plan**.

### 5.1 Every review starts with a safety check (first 5 minutes)

Every review (and every handover) starts with a quick safety check **before** you touch the chart:

1. **Is the mother well?** Colour, distress, bleeding, fits, urge to push. Pulse now (not the last recorded value).
2. **Is the baby well?** Listen to the FHR for a full minute **straight after a contraction**, with a hand on the mother's radial pulse.
3. **Is anything running?** Oxytocin drip (rate, concentration), MgSO₄, IV fluids. If there are **more than 5 contractions in 10 min** or the FHR is abnormal, **stop the oxytocin now**.
4. **Look at the chart**: when was the last VE, FHR, BP, temperature and urine? Is any value already beyond the **alert line**, the **action line**, or an **LCG alert threshold** without a written plan?
5. **Call a senior at once** for: FHR below 110 or above 160 or late decelerations, cord prolapse, thick meconium, bleeding, BP 160/110 or more or fits, maternal pulse 120 or more, crossing the **action line**, or signs of obstruction.

> [!REDFLAG]
> An empty partograph is itself a red flag. If the last FHR was recorded 2 hours ago, the baby has effectively not been monitored for 2 hours; listen now and document the time honestly.

### 5.2 Examination at each review (Hutchison order)

Follow the Hutchison sequence and examine in the **order the chart is filled**, always abdomen **before** VE.

1. **Introduce yourself, confirm identity, explain and gain consent**; offer a chaperone.
2. **Hand hygiene.**
3. **Position and exposure**: left lateral tilt or semi-recumbent; expose the abdomen only.
4. **General inspection from the end of the bed**: distress, exhaustion, colour (pallor, cyanosis), hydration (dry lips), bleeding on the pad, fits, pushing.
5. **Vital signs**: pulse (every 30 min), BP (4-hourly), temperature (2-hourly) on the modified WHO partograph.
6. **Hands, face and neck**: pallor, oedema (hands and face in pre-eclampsia), dry mucosa.
7. **Obstetric abdominal examination**:
   - **Inspection**: shape, scars (previous CS), **Bandl's ring** (obstruction), full bladder.
   - **Fundal height** and **lie**.
   - **Presentation**.
   - **Engagement / descent in fifths palpable** (plotted as **O**). Always abdominally before every VE.
   - **Contractions**: palpate for **10 full minutes**; count and time each.
   - **Auscultation of the fetal heart** for 60 s after a contraction (section 5.5).
8. **VE** (every 4 h, or sooner if indicated): dilatation (X), liquor, moulding, position, station, caput; exclude cord.
9. **Bladder and urine**: volume, protein, acetone, blood.
10. **Complete, thank the woman, cover her, hand hygiene**, then **plot, interpret and write an assessment and plan**.

| Order | Examination | What to record | Key abnormal finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pulse, BP, temperature | Pulse every 30 min, BP 4-hourly, temperature 2-hourly (modified WHO partograph) | Pulse 120 or more; BP 140/90 or more; temperature 38 degrees C or more (LCG alert 37.5 or more) |
| 2 | **Descent in fifths** (abdominal) | O on the cervicograph | Head still 4/5-5/5 in active labour |
| 3 | **Contractions** by palpation for **10 full minutes** | Number of boxes shaded; dots under 20 s, diagonal 20-40 s, solid over 40 s | 2 or fewer in 10 min (inadequate) or more than 5 (tachysystole) |
| 4 | **FHR** for 60 s after a contraction | Dot on the FHR graph | Below 110, above 160, late decelerations |
| 5 | **VE** (every 4 h, or sooner if indicated) | X for dilatation; liquor (I, C, M, B, A); moulding (0 to +++); position; station; caput | Cervix not advancing; moulding +++; thick meconium; cord felt; oedematous cervix |
| 6 | Bladder and urine | Volume, protein, acetone | Full bladder; ketones; protein; haematuria (obstruction) |

**Making each plot accurate**

- **Descent**: always by **abdominal fifths** (not station alone; station can be fooled by caput).
- **Contractions**: palpate for a full 10 minutes; do not rely on the mother's report.
- **Cervix**: the same examiner at each VE reduces variability; if you are a student, have the midwife confirm.
- **Position**: identify the posterior fontanelle; an OP position explains slow progress and often a backache.

### 5.3 Plotting the modified WHO partograph

The chart has three parts from top to bottom: **fetal condition, labour progress, maternal condition**, plus a box for drugs and fluids.

**Step 1. Patient information (top).** Name, age, gravida, para, hospital number, **date and time of admission**, **time of rupture of membranes**, and gestational age.

**Step 2. Fetal condition.**

1. **Fetal heart rate**: every **30 minutes** in the active first stage (WHO 2018: every 15-30 minutes). Listen for **a full minute immediately after a contraction**. Plot as a dot on the FHR graph (scale 80-200 bpm; the band 110-160 is often shaded) and join the dots.
2. **Liquor** at every VE (and whenever seen):
   - **I** = membranes intact
   - **C** = ruptured, clear liquor
   - **M** = meconium-stained (grade it: M1 thin, M2, M3 thick)
   - **B** = blood-stained
   - **A** = absent (ruptured, no liquor draining)
3. **Moulding** at every VE: **0** bones separate, sutures felt easily; **+** bones just touching; **++** overlapping but can be reduced by gentle pressure; **+++** overlapping and not reducible.

**Step 3. Progress of labour.**

1. **Cervical dilatation**: the graph has 0-10 cm on the vertical axis and hours on the horizontal axis. Plot with an **X**.
   - The **first active-phase plot (4 cm or more) goes ON the alert line**, at the matching dilatation. For example, if admitted at 6 cm, put the X at 6 cm on the alert line and write the actual clock time underneath.
   - If a woman was in the latent phase and then reaches 4 cm, the X is **transferred to the alert line** (draw a broken line to show the transfer).
   - Join successive Xs with a line.
2. **Descent of the head** by **abdominal palpation in fifths** (5/5 = fully above the brim, 0/5 = fully in the pelvis). Plot with an **O** on the same grid (the 0-5 descent scale is on the same axis). **Palpate abdominally before every VE.**
3. **Hours**: write the actual clock time under each column.
4. **Contractions**: every **30 minutes**, count the number of contractions in **10 minutes** and time the duration of each. Shade one box per contraction:
   - **Dots**: less than 20 seconds (mild)
   - **Diagonal lines**: 20-40 seconds (moderate)
   - **Solid shading**: more than 40 seconds (strong)

**Step 4. Drugs and fluids.**

- **Oxytocin**: record the concentration (units per litre) and the drip rate (drops/min) every 30 minutes.
- **Other drugs and IV fluids**: record every dose (antibiotics, MgSO₄, analgesia).

**Step 5. Maternal condition.**

| Parameter | Frequency (modified WHO partograph) | Plot |
|---|---|---|
| Pulse | Every 30 min | Dot |
| BP | Every 4 h | Arrow (systolic top, diastolic bottom) |
| Temperature | Every 2 h | Number |
| Urine: volume, protein, acetone | Every time passed | Record |

**Step 6. Vaginal examinations.** **Every 4 hours** in the active phase, or earlier if: signs of second stage, abnormal FHR, rupture of membranes (to exclude cord prolapse), bleeding, or before a decision (augmentation, referral). Each VE records cervix, liquor, moulding, station, position and caput.

### 5.4 Using the WHO Labour Care Guide (LCG) 2020

**Structure (7 sections)**

1. **Identifying information and labour characteristics at admission**: parity, labour onset (spontaneous/induced), active labour diagnosis date/time, ruptured membranes date/time, risk factors.
2. **Supportive care**: companion (Y/N), pain relief (Y/N), oral fluid (Y/N), posture (supine or mobile).
3. **Care of the baby**: baseline FHR, FHR decelerations (No, Early, Late, Variable), amniotic fluid, fetal position, caput, moulding.
4. **Care of the woman**: pulse, systolic BP, diastolic BP, temperature, urine (protein, acetone).
5. **Labour progress**: contractions per 10 min, duration of contractions, cervix (plotted as X from 5 cm), descent (plotted as O).
6. **Medication**: oxytocin (units per litre, drops/min), medicines, IV fluids.
7. **Shared decision-making**: **Assessment** and **Plan** written at each review.

**How it works**

- Starts in the **active first stage at 5 cm**. Observations are written in boxes (numbers or codes), not only as a graph.
- Each row has an **alert threshold** printed in the "Alert" column. If a recorded value meets the threshold, you **circle/highlight it**, and it must lead to an assessment and a documented plan (and usually a senior review).
- For cervical dilatation, instead of an alert line, there is a **time limit for each centimetre**: if the woman stays at that dilatation for the stated time or longer, it is an alert.

**LCG monitoring frequencies (active first stage)**

| Parameter | Frequency |
|---|---|
| FHR, decelerations | Every 30 min (every 5 min in second stage) |
| Contractions (frequency and duration) | Every 30 min |
| Pulse | Hourly (more often if abnormal, per local protocol) [VERIFY: confirm pulse frequency against the LCG user's manual] |
| BP, temperature | Every 4 h |
| Urine | Each void |
| VE (cervix, descent, liquor, position, caput, moulding) | Every 4 h |
| Supportive care | Every hour |

> [!NOTE]
> Local adaptations exist. The Uganda customisation studies simplified some sections and kept the core alert thresholds. Follow the form and schedule printed on the version your unit uses.

### 5.5 Intermittent auscultation of the fetal heart (the Ugandan standard)

1. Palpate to find the fetal back (anterior shoulder area); place the Pinard or Doppler there.
2. **Palpate the maternal pulse at the same time** so you do not count the mother's heart rate by mistake (especially with a Doppler, and when the mother is tachycardic).
3. Listen **during the last part of a contraction and for at least 60 seconds after it ends** (to detect late decelerations).
4. Record the **baseline rate**, rhythm, and any **decelerations** or accelerations.

| Stage | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Latent phase | At least every hour (per local protocol) |
| Active first stage | Every 15-30 min (WHO 2018); every 30 min on the partograph |
| Second stage | Every 5 min, or after every contraction |

## 6. Findings and Interpretation

### Reading the lines (the geometry)

The alert line rises 1 cm per hour from the first active-phase plot, so the alert line reaches **dilatation *d* at (*d* − 4) hours after the start** (for a 4 cm start). The action line reaches the same dilatation **4 hours later**. So a woman who is at dilatation *d* at time *t*:

- is **on or left of the alert line** if she reached *d* on time (normal);
- is **between the lines** if she is up to 4 h behind;
- is **at or beyond the action line** if she is 4 h or more behind.

| Position on the chart | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| On or left of the alert line | Normal progress | Continue monitoring and supportive care |
| Crossed the alert line | Slower than expected | **HC III: refer now.** Hospital/HC IV: reassess the 3 Ps, hydrate, empty bladder, mobilise, ARM if appropriate, monitor more closely |
| At or beyond the action line | Prolonged active phase | Senior review and a decision: augment, assisted vaginal delivery or caesarean |

> [!PEARL]
> The descent O and the dilatation X tell different stories. **X rising but O not falling** (e.g. 8 cm but head still 3/5) means the cervix is opening around a head that is not coming down: think CPD or malposition, not "good progress".

> Examiners love the LCG cervical thresholds. Memorise "**6-5-3-2.5-2**" for 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 cm.

### Fetal heart rate interpretation

| Finding | Meaning / common causes |
|---|---|
| **Baseline 110-160 bpm**, regular, no decelerations | Normal/reassuring |
| **Tachycardia above 160** | Maternal fever/chorioamnionitis, dehydration, maternal tachycardia, drugs, early hypoxia, fetal anaemia |
| **Bradycardia below 110** (sustained) | Cord compression/prolapse, tachysystole, abruption, uterine rupture, maternal hypotension (supine, spinal); a rate below 100 for over 3 min is an emergency |
| **Early decelerations** (dip with the contraction, mirror image) | Head compression; usually benign |
| **Late decelerations** (dip starts after the peak, recovers late) | **Uteroplacental insufficiency / hypoxia** |
| **Variable decelerations** | Cord compression; worrying if deep, prolonged or with slow recovery |

**CTG: when and why not routine.** WHO 2018 does **not recommend admission CTG or continuous CTG for low-risk women**, because it increases caesarean and instrumental deliveries without improving newborn outcomes. CTG is useful in high-risk labours (previous CS, pre-eclampsia, oxytocin augmentation, meconium, abnormal IA) where available. Most Ugandan public units do not have it; learn IA well.

### Liquor, moulding and maternal findings

| Finding | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **M** (especially M3 / M+++, thick) | Possible fetal hypoxia; risk of meconium aspiration; closer FHR watch, prepare for resuscitation |
| **B** | Abruption or vasa praevia until proved otherwise |
| **A** | Oligohydramnios; cord compression risk |
| Moulding **++** | Watch for CPD |
| Moulding **+++** with caput +++ and head 3/5 or more | **CPD / obstruction** |
| Pulse rising | Haemorrhage, sepsis, dehydration, exhaustion; in a scarred uterus, think **rupture** |
| Temperature 37.5 (LCG) / 38 degrees C or more | Chorioamnionitis, malaria |
| Urine acetone | Dehydration/ketosis (poor contractions) |
| Urine protein | Pre-eclampsia (or contamination) |
| Haematuria, low output | Obstructed labour (bladder compression) |

### Red flags on the chart

> [!REDFLAG]
> - Dilatation crosses the **alert line** (HC III: refer) or reaches the **action line** (full assessment and decision)
> - LCG alert on **cervical time thresholds** or second-stage duration
> - FHR below 110 or above 160, or late decelerations
> - Thick meconium (M3/M+++) or blood-stained liquor
> - Moulding +++ with caput +++ and a head 3/5 or more above the brim
> - More than 5 contractions in 10 minutes, or contractions lasting more than 60 seconds (especially on oxytocin)
> - Maternal pulse 120 or more, temperature 37.5 or more (LCG) / 38 or more, BP 140/90 or more, protein ++, acetone ++

### Bedside tests that support the chart

| Tool | How | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| **Pinard / hand-held Doppler** | 60 s after a contraction, maternal pulse palpated at the same time | Baseline FHR and decelerations; avoids counting the maternal pulse |
| **Watch with a second hand** | For contractions and the FHR | Accurate counts; never estimate |
| **Urine dipstick** | Each void | Protein (pre-eclampsia), acetone (dehydration/ketosis), blood (obstruction) |
| **Measuring jug / catheter** | Measure each void | Low output in dehydration, obstruction, or pre-eclampsia |
| **HemoCue** | If pale, bleeding or known anaemia | Plan for blood before delivery |
| **Bedside ultrasound** | Position uncertain, FHR not heard, suspected malpresentation | Confirms OP/OT, breech, twins or IUFD |
| **CTG** (where available) | High-risk labour or abnormal intermittent auscultation | Continuous trace; not for routine low-risk women (WHO 2018) |

### Laboratory and imaging when the chart raises a question

Most monitoring is at the bedside; the laboratory is used when the chart raises a question.

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hb / FBC** | Admission if no recent Hb; bleeding; prolonged labour; fever | Hb 11 g/dL or more; raised WBC is normal in labour, so interpret with fever and clinical signs | Anaemia → crossmatch and deliver where blood is available. HemoCue at HC III/IV; FBC at hospitals |
| **Group and crossmatch** | Crossed the action line, planned augmentation in a high-risk woman, CS likely | Blood ready | Needed before CS; HC III must refer (no blood) |
| **Malaria RDT / smear** | Temperature 37.5 degrees C or more (LCG alert) or 38 or more | Positive | Treat malaria; still look for chorioamnionitis |
| **Urinalysis / culture** | Protein ++ or fever | Proteinuria; infection | Pre-eclampsia workup or antibiotics. Culture only at regional/national referral |
| **Platelets, LFTs, creatinine** | BP 140/90 or more with protein or symptoms | Low platelets, raised ALT/AST or creatinine | Severe pre-eclampsia/HELLP → MgSO₄, delivery plan. Regional/national referral |
| **Ultrasound** | Slow progress with uncertain position, suspected malpresentation, FHR not found | OP/OT, brow, breech, IUFD | Explains slow progress; changes mode of delivery. Most HC IVs and hospitals, operator-dependent |
| **CTG** | Abnormal IA, oxytocin augmentation, previous CS, meconium (where available) | Normal / suspicious / pathological | Expedite delivery if pathological. Mengo, Mulago/Kawempe, some regional hospitals only |
| **Cord blood gas** | After delivery of a compromised baby (national referral only) | Acidosis | Documents intrapartum hypoxia; rarely available in Uganda |

## 7. Clinical Acumen

### Thinking like a clinician: interpreting slow progress

1. **Check the plot is correct** (started in the active phase? times right? same examiner?).
2. **Mother**: is she dehydrated, ketotic, exhausted, febrile? Is the bladder full?
3. **Powers**: count contractions; are they adequate (3-5 in 10 min, 40 s or longer)?
4. **Passenger**: position (OP/OT), deflexion, malpresentation, size; moulding and caput.
5. **Passage**: pelvic assessment; is the head still high?
6. **Baby**: FHR and liquor.

**Decision matrix at the action line (or LCG cervical alert):**

| Findings | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Contractions inadequate, no moulding/minimal caput, head descending, **primigravida**, FHR normal | Inefficient uterine activity | Hydrate, empty bladder, ARM if membranes intact, then **oxytocin augmentation** (hospital/HC IV with CS backup), reassess in 2-4 h |
| Contractions adequate, **moulding +++, big caput, head high** | CPD / obstruction | **Caesarean section** |
| **Multipara** with slow progress | Obstruction until proven otherwise | Do not give oxytocin without senior review; usually CS |
| **FHR abnormal** at any point | Fetal compromise | Intrauterine resuscitation, then expedite delivery (vacuum if fully dilated and head low; otherwise CS) |
| Malpresentation found (brow, face mento-posterior, transverse lie) | Mechanical | Caesarean section |

### If X, then think Y

- **X rising, O static** → the cervix is opening around a head that is not descending: CPD or malposition.
- **A slow multipara** → obstruction until proved otherwise; oxytocin only after senior review.
- **Doppler reading of 88 bpm** → check the maternal pulse first; you may be counting the mother.
- **Fetal tachycardia with maternal fever** → chorioamnionitis or malaria; look for the source as well as the baby.
- **Rising maternal pulse in a woman with a previous CS** → scar rupture until proved otherwise.
- **Haematuria with a high head** → obstructed labour.
- **Tachysystole on oxytocin** → stop the drip; the half-life is a few minutes.

### When to stop and escalate

- **Call a senior immediately**: abnormal FHR, cord prolapse, thick meconium, bleeding, BP 160/110 or more, fits, pulse 120 or more, action line reached, signs of obstruction.
- **Do not wait for the action line** when there are signs of obstruction (moulding +++, high head, haematuria, Bandl's ring).
- **At an HC III, the alert line is the referral line.**

### Worked examples

**Model one-line summary from a chart**: "Mrs A., P0+0 at 40 weeks, admitted at 4 cm at 08:00; now at 12:00 she is 5 cm, head 4/5, moulding ++, contractions 3 in 10 lasting 30 s, FHR 140, liquor clear; she has **crossed the alert line** and is **one hour from the action line**, mother and fetus well."

**Problem list**: (1) slow active phase (alert line crossed); (2) inadequate contractions; (3) moulding ++ (watch for CPD); (4) mother and fetus currently well.

**Working diagnosis**: "Prolonged active phase in a primigravida, probably due to inadequate uterine activity, CPD not yet excluded."

> [!EXAM]
> **Worked example 1: crossing the lines.** Admitted at **08:00 at 4 cm** (X on the alert line at 08:00). The alert line reaches 5 cm at 09:00 and 10 cm at 14:00; the action line reaches 5 cm at 13:00 and 10 cm at 18:00.
> - **12:00: VE 5 cm**, head 4/5, 3 contractions in 10 min each 30 s (diagonal shading), moulding +, liquor clear, FHR 136. She is **3 h right of the alert line**, **1 h before the action line**.
> - **Interpretation**: slow progress with **inadequate contractions** (not 3-5 lasting more than 40 s), no marked moulding, fetus well.
> - **Plan**: at an **HC III, refer now** (alert line crossed). At hospital: rehydrate, empty the bladder, mobilise, **ARM** if membranes intact (and no contraindication), senior review; if still inadequate at the action line and no CPD → **oxytocin augmentation**; reassess in 2-4 h.

> [!EXAM]
> **Worked example 2: obstruction before the action line.** A P4 is admitted at **10:00 at 6 cm** (X on the alert line at 6 cm). At **14:00**: cervix **7 cm**, head **3/5**, **moulding +++**, **caput ++**, 4 contractions in 10 min each 50 s (solid shading), urine has blood, FHR 164.
> - **Plot**: the alert line reached 7 cm at 11:00, so she is 3 h behind (between the lines).
> - **Interpretation**: adequate powers, a head that is **not descending**, **non-reducible moulding**, haematuria and fetal tachycardia in a **multipara** = **obstructed labour / CPD**. You do **not** wait for the action line.
> - **Plan**: no oxytocin; catheterise, IV fluids, antibiotics, crossmatch, **emergency caesarean section** (or immediate referral with an escort from an HC III).

> [!EXAM]
> **Worked example 3: the FHR and the LCG.** On the LCG, a primigravida has been at **7 cm since 09:00**; it is now 12:00 (3 h at 7 cm = **LCG cervical alert**). The FHR baseline is 145, but after each contraction it falls to 105 and returns to baseline about 40 s after the contraction has ended.
> - **Interpretation**: a **late deceleration** (circle "L": an LCG alert) plus a **progress alert**.
> - **Plan**: intrauterine resuscitation (stop oxytocin, left lateral, IV fluids, VE to exclude cord prolapse and assess), listen over the next 3 contractions; persistent late decelerations with a cervix at 7 cm → **caesarean section**. Write the **assessment and plan** in the shared decision-making section.

**Oxytocin arithmetic you must be able to do**: 2.5 IU in 500 mL = **5 mIU/mL**. With a **20-drop/mL** giving set, 10 drops/min = 0.5 mL/min = **2.5 mIU/min**. Record the IU/L and drops/min on the chart every 30 minutes.

### Common student mistakes

- Plotting the **first VE of 5-6 cm at 4 cm on the time axis** instead of **on the alert line**.
- Plotting descent as **station** instead of fifths.
- Recording contractions from the mother's report instead of palpation.
- Counting the **maternal pulse** as the FHR on a Doppler.
- Listening to the FHR **between** contractions only (misses late decelerations).
- Writing values but not writing an **assessment and plan** when an alert appears.
- Filling the partograph after delivery.

## 8. Complications and Troubleshooting

### What to do when the partograph or LCG gives an alert

**At the alert line (modified WHO partograph)**

1. **HC III** (no theatre): **refer** to an HC IV/hospital with the partograph, referral note, IV line and escort. Do not wait for the action line.
2. **Hospital/HC IV**: re-examine the woman (the 3 Ps), correct dehydration and bladder, encourage mobility, ARM if membranes intact and no contraindication (for example HIV with a detectable viral load), and monitor more closely.

**At the action line**

1. Senior review. Full assessment: maternal condition, fetal condition, contractions, pelvis, head.
2. **Decide**: oxytocin augmentation (if contractions are inadequate and there is no obstruction), **caesarean section** (obstruction, fetal distress not near delivery, malpresentation), or **assisted vaginal delivery** (fully dilated, head 0/5-1/5 and within reach).
3. Document the decision and time.

### Oxytocin augmentation (hospital/HC IV only, with continuous one-to-one monitoring)

| Drug | Dilution | Starting rate | Increase | Maximum | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oxytocin** | **2.5 IU in 500 mL** normal saline or Ringer's lactate (5 IU/L) | **10 drops/min** (about 2.5 mIU/min with a 20-drop giving set) | By 10 drops/min every **30 min** until 3-5 contractions in 10 min, each over 40 s | 60 drops/min. If still inadequate: **5 IU in 500 mL** (10 IU/L) at 30 drops/min, increasing by 10 drops/min every 30 min to 60 drops/min. If still inadequate: **multiparas and previous CS go to caesarean**; only in **primigravidae** may a 10 IU in 500 mL solution be tried (WHO MCPC regimen) | **Contraindicated** in obstruction, previous classical CS, malpresentation, fetal distress. Use with great caution in multiparas and previous lower-segment CS |

> [!DRUG]
> Stop the oxytocin immediately if there are **more than 5 contractions in 10 minutes**, contractions lasting **more than 60 seconds**, or **fetal distress**. Turn the woman onto her left side. If hyperstimulation persists, consider **terbutaline 250 micrograms SC** (where available). Oxytocin has a half-life of only a few minutes, so stopping the drip works quickly.

> [!UGANDA]
> Oxytocin augmentation should only be done where caesarean section is available (HC IV and hospitals) and a health worker can stay with the woman. It is one of the commonest contributors to **ruptured uterus** in Uganda when used in multiparas or in obstructed labour.

### Managing an abnormal FHR (intrauterine resuscitation)

1. **Stop oxytocin** if running.
2. **Left lateral position** (relieves aortocaval compression).
3. **IV fluids**: Ringer's lactate or normal saline (for example 500 mL-1 L) if hypotensive or dehydrated.
4. **VE**: exclude **cord prolapse**, check progress (can she deliver quickly?).
5. **Treat causes**: maternal fever (paracetamol 1 g oral, antibiotics if chorioamnionitis), hypotension, tachysystole (tocolysis if needed).
6. **Re-listen for at least 3 contractions**. If persistently abnormal: **expedite delivery**: vacuum if fully dilated and head low, otherwise caesarean section.
7. Prepare for **neonatal resuscitation** (call the person skilled in bag and mask, check equipment).

### Managing prolonged second stage

- Nullipara over 3 h (or over 2 h active pushing), multipara over 2 h (or over 1 h pushing): assess for **malposition, CPD, exhaustion, inadequate contractions**.
- Options: rehydrate, empty bladder, change position, oxytocin if contractions inadequate (not in multiparas without senior review), **vacuum extraction** if the head is 0/5-1/5 and the position is known, or **caesarean** if the head is high.

### Troubleshooting the tool itself

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| FHR not heard | Reposition over the fetal back; check Doppler battery and gel; palpate the maternal pulse; bedside ultrasound to exclude IUFD or confirm position |
| Doppler rate equals the maternal pulse | You are counting the mother: palpate the radial pulse at the same time and move the probe |
| Woman arrives from a lower facility with a partograph | Continue it; transfer times and findings; do not start "fresh" |
| Latent-phase woman plotted on the active graph | Re-plot: transfer the X to the alert line when she reaches 4 cm (broken line) |
| Different examiners give different dilatations | Same examiner where possible; students have the midwife confirm |

### Complications of failure to monitor or act

| Missed finding | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Slow progress not acted on | Obstructed labour, **ruptured uterus**, **obstetric fistula**, maternal death |
| FHR abnormalities missed | Intrapartum stillbirth, **birth asphyxia/HIE**, cerebral palsy |
| Fever/prolonged ROM ignored | Chorioamnionitis, puerperal sepsis, neonatal sepsis |
| Hypertension missed | Eclampsia, stroke |
| Oxytocin not recorded/titrated | Tachysystole, fetal distress, uterine rupture |
| Over-reaction to slow early labour | Unnecessary oxytocin, ARM and caesarean |

## 9. Documentation and Presenting Findings

- The partograph/LCG **goes with the woman** on referral.
- Write each **assessment and plan** at the time. The partograph is a legal document and a key part of **maternal and perinatal death reviews (MPDSR)**.
- On the LCG, every circled alert must have a matching **assessment** and **plan** in the shared decision-making section.

**Model presentation using the partograph**

"Mrs B.N., 26 years, G2P1, at 40 weeks, in labour. She was admitted at 08:00 at **5 cm**, plotted on the alert line. At 12:00 she was **9 cm**, head 1/5, moulding +, liquor clear, contractions **4 in 10 lasting 45 seconds**, FHR 130-145 throughout, pulse 80-90, BP 120/70, afebrile, passing urine without ketones. She is **on the alert line** (the alert line runs from 5 cm at 08:00 to 9 cm at 12:00). **Assessment: normal progress with mother and fetus in good condition. Plan: continue monitoring, re-examine when she has an urge to push or if any alert appears, supportive care, prepare for delivery and AMTSL.**"

**Presenting an abnormal chart (structure)**: identity and parity → time of admission and dilatation → current dilatation, descent, moulding, caput → contractions → FHR and liquor → maternal vitals and urine → **where she is relative to the lines** (hours behind) → assessment (the 3 Ps) → plan.

## 11. Key Points

### High-yield points

- The partograph is a **decision tool, not a form**: every abnormal value needs a written plan.
- Start in the **active phase only**: 4 cm (modified WHO partograph), 5 cm (LCG).
- The first active-phase X goes **on the alert line**; write the real clock time below.
- Alert line 1 cm/h; action line 4 h to the right. **HC III: the alert line is the referral line.**
- Descent in **fifths** (O), cervix X; **X rising but O static = CPD/malposition**.
- FHR **110-160**, listened to for **60 s after a contraction** with the maternal pulse palpated.
- LCG cervical alerts **6-5-3-2.5-2** h at 5-9 cm; second stage 3 h (nullipara) / 2 h (multipara).
- Obstruction (moulding +++, high head, haematuria) = act **before** the action line.
- Oxytocin only where CS is available; stop for >5 contractions in 10 min, >60 s or fetal distress.
- A slow multipara is obstructed until proved otherwise.
- WHO 2018: no routine admission or continuous CTG for low-risk women.

| Start LCG | **5 cm** |

| Action line | **4 h to the right** of the alert line |
| FHR (first stage / second stage) | **Every 30 min** / **every 5 min** (after each contraction); normal **110-160 bpm** |

### Exam traps

- **"Admitted at 6 cm: plot the X at 6 cm above the zero-hour mark."** → Wrong. The first active-phase X goes **on the alert line** at 6 cm; write the real clock time beneath.
- **"Plot descent as station."** → Wrong. Descent is plotted as **fifths palpable abdominally** (O); station can be fooled by caput.
- **"She hasn't reached the action line, so obstruction can wait."** → Wrong. Moulding +++, a high head and haematuria mean **act now**, wherever the X is.
- **"Crossed the alert line at an HC III: wait for the action line."** → Wrong. At a facility without theatre, the alert line **is** the referral line.
- **"Slow progress → oxytocin."** → Only after excluding obstruction and malpresentation, and only where CS is available; **multiparas and previous CS need senior review**.
- **"Listen to the FHR between contractions."** → Wrong. Listen during the end of a contraction and **for 60 s after it**, or you will miss late decelerations.
- **"Start the partograph at 2 cm to be safe."** → Wrong. Starting in the latent phase labels normal women as slow and drives unnecessary interventions.
- **"A Doppler reading of 88 bpm is fetal bradycardia."** → Check the **maternal pulse** first; you may be counting the mother.

### Questions seniors ask

**Viva questions**

1. **Where do you plot the first VE of 6 cm?** On the alert line at 6 cm, and write the actual time below.
2. **What is the distance between the alert and action lines, and why?** 4 hours; originally to allow time for referral and simple interventions.
3. **What does crossing the alert line mean at an HC III?** Refer to a facility with CS capability.
4. **How do you record a contraction lasting 30 seconds?** A box with diagonal lines.
5. **What is the significance of moulding +++?** Bones overlapping and not reducible: suggests CPD.
6. **Why listen after a contraction?** To detect late decelerations (uteroplacental insufficiency).
7. **What are the LCG cervical alert thresholds?** 5 cm 6 h, 6 cm 5 h, 7 cm 3 h, 8 cm 2.5 h, 9 cm 2 h.
8. **Why did WHO move away from the 1 cm/hour rule?** It classified many normal women as slow and drove unnecessary interventions; newer evidence shows slower normal progress, especially before 5-6 cm.
9. **When is oxytocin contraindicated when the chart shows slow progress?** Obstruction/CPD, malpresentation, fetal distress, previous classical CS; high caution in grand multiparas.

**On the ward round**

1. **"Where is she relative to the lines?"** State the time at which the alert line reached her current dilatation, and so how many hours she is behind (0 = on the alert line; 4 or more = at or beyond the action line).
2. **"Why is she slow?"** Go through the 3 Ps plus the plot: contractions (count them), passenger (position, moulding, caput, descent), passage (pelvis), and whether the chart was started correctly.
3. **"What does moulding +++ mean?"** Overlapping skull bones that cannot be reduced: CPD until proved otherwise.
4. **"How did you record these contractions?"** Number of boxes = number in 10 min; shading by duration (dots under 20 s, diagonal 20-40 s, solid over 40 s).
5. **"What is your oxytocin rate in mIU/min?"** 2.5 IU/500 mL = 5 mIU/mL; with 20 drops/mL, 10 drops/min ≈ 2.5 mIU/min.
6. **"What would make you stop the oxytocin?"** More than 5 contractions in 10 min, a contraction lasting over 60 s, abnormal FHR, or signs of obstruction or rupture.
7. **"What is the difference between the partograph and the LCG?"** The LCG starts at 5 cm, replaces the alert/action lines with time-at-each-centimetre alerts, adds supportive care, and requires a written assessment and plan for every alert.

### Mnemonics

- **Liquor codes**: "**I C M B A**" = Intact, Clear, Meconium, Blood, Absent ("I Can Monitor Babies Always").
- **Contraction shading**: "**Dots are Dainty, Lines are Light-moderate, Solid is Strong**" (under 20 s, 20-40 s, over 40 s).
- **LCG cervix thresholds**: "**6-5-3-2.5-2**" at 5-6-7-8-9 cm.
- **Plotting symbols**: "**X marks the cervix, O is the head**" (O looks like the round head going down).
- **Frequencies**: "**30-30-30, 2, 4, 4**": FHR, contractions and pulse every 30 min; temperature every 2 h; BP and VE every 4 h.
- **Slow progress**: "**3 Ps + P**": Powers, Passenger, Passage, and Plot (check the plot is right).

### OSCE tips

- You may be given a **partially completed partograph** and asked to interpret it. Work top to bottom: fetal condition, progress, contractions, drugs, maternal condition. Then give a **one-line summary** and a **plan**.
- When asked "what next?", always start with "I would reassess the woman" and name the 3 Ps.
- State where she is relative to the lines **in hours**, not just "she has crossed".
- In a plotting station, say aloud why the first X goes on the alert line.
