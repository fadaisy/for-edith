## 1. Purpose and Indications

The **newborn examination** is a structured, head-to-toe check that answers three questions:

1. **Is this baby well now?** (danger signs, vital signs, feeding)
2. **Is anything abnormal that needs action or referral?** (malformations, hips, eyes, heart, birth injury, infection risk)
3. **Is the baby the expected size for gestation?** (weight, length, head circumference, Ballard score)

UCG 2023 (section 17.1.1) asks every health worker to examine all babies after delivery, before discharge, and whenever a baby is seen in the first week.

Why it matters in Uganda: many babies go home within hours of birth, so this may be the **only full examination** a baby gets. Missing a danger sign (sepsis, jaundice, hypothermia), a cleft palate (feeding failure), an imperforate anus, a dislocated hip or a white pupil (retinoblastoma) has lifelong consequences.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **observe before you touch, and keep the baby warm**. Most of the examination (colour, breathing, posture, movements) is done by looking at a calm baby; examine in a warm room, uncover one part at a time, and finish with the things that make babies cry (hips, Moro, mouth).

### When to examine

| Time | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **At birth (first minute and first hour)** | Breathing, HR, colour, tone, obvious malformations; Apgar; skin-to-skin; vitamin K, eye ointment, cord care |
| **Within 24 h and before discharge** | Full head-to-toe examination, measurements, Ballard if gestation uncertain, hips, red reflex, palate, anus, spine, femoral pulses, feeding observation |
| **3–7 days** (postnatal visit) | Jaundice, feeding, weight, cord, eyes, skin, danger signs |
| **6 weeks** | Repeat hips, red reflex, heart, testes; growth; development (social smile); immunisation |
| **Any sick visit in the first month** | Danger-sign assessment (IMNCI) |

## 2. Contraindications and Precautions

- **Defer a full examination** while a baby is being resuscitated or is unstable: stabilise first (ABC, warmth, glucose).
- **Do not perform Barlow or Ortolani forcefully or repeatedly** (can damage the hip), and **avoid them** in a baby with a suspected limb fracture.
- **Do not force the legs** of a baby born by breech (UCG).
- In **suspected neonatal tetanus**, minimise stimulation (examine after sedation).
- **Hypothermia**: uncover one part at a time; keep the examination short in small babies.
- **Breast engorgement**: never squeeze the breasts (abscess risk).
- Always obtain the **mother's permission** and explain what you are doing.

## 3. Relevant Anatomy and Normal Values

### Normal values for a term newborn

| Parameter | Normal range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight** | **2.5–4.0 kg** | Below 2.5 kg = LBW; above 4.0 kg = macrosomia (think maternal diabetes). Plot by gestation: SGA below the 10th centile, LGA above the 90th |
| **Length** | About **48–53 cm** | Use a length board (two people) |
| **Head circumference (OFC)** | About **33–37 cm** (mean about 35 cm) | Largest of 3 measurements over the occiput and above the eyebrows; microcephaly below **−2 SD** (severe below −3 SD) |
| **Respiratory rate** | **30–60/min** | Count for a full minute when calm (UCG); periodic breathing (pauses under 20 s) is normal |
| **Heart rate** | **100–160/min** | Sleep can be 90–100; crying up to 180 |
| **Temperature** | **36.5–37.5 °C** axillary | Hypothermia below 36.5 °C |
| **SpO₂** | **95% or more** after the first hours | Pre- and post-ductal difference 3% or less |
| **Capillary refill** | Under **3 seconds** | Press on the sternum |
| **Weight change** | Loss up to **7–10%** in the first 3–5 days; regain birth weight by **10–14 days** | More than 10% = feeding problem |
| **First urine** | Within **24 h** | |
| **First meconium** | Within **24 h** (almost all term babies by 48 h) | Delay = obstruction until proven otherwise |

### Head, face and body landmarks

| Structure | Normal |
|---|---|
| **Anterior fontanelle** | Diamond-shaped, about **1–4 cm**, soft and flat; closes by **12–18 months** |
| **Posterior fontanelle** | Small triangle; closes by about **6–8 weeks** |
| **Ears** | Top of the ear in line with the outer canthus |
| **Umbilical cord** | **2 arteries and 1 vein** |
| **Liver** | Edge up to **2 cm** below the costal margin |
| **Posture (term)** | Flexed, symmetrical spontaneous movements |
| **Peripheral colour** | Acrocyanosis (blue hands and feet) is normal in the first 24–48 h |
| **Pull-to-sit (term)** | Some head lag, but not floppy |
| **Ventral suspension (term)** | Holds the head briefly in line with the trunk, limbs flexed |

### Gestational age categories

| Category | Gestation |
|---|---|
| Preterm | Below 37 weeks |
| Term | 37+0 to 41+6 weeks |
| Post-term | 42 weeks or more |

### Danger signs (IMNCI / UCG)

Not feeding well, convulsions, RR 60 or more or below 30, grunting, severe chest indrawing, central cyanosis, temperature above 37.5 °C or below 35.5 °C, floppy or no spontaneous movement, jaundice in the first 24 h or yellow palms and soles.

## 4. Equipment and Preparation

### Equipment

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| **Warm, well-lit room**, clean surface or radiant warmer | Prevent hypothermia; see colour and jaundice |
| **Hand-washing facilities or alcohol rub, gloves** | Infection prevention |
| **Weighing scale** (zeroed, 10–20 g accuracy) | Weight |
| **Non-stretch tape measure** | OFC (and length if no board) |
| **Length board** (infantometer) | Length |
| **Thermometer** (digital, low-reading) | Temperature |
| **Stethoscope** (neonatal or small paediatric) | Heart and lungs |
| **Watch with a second hand** | RR, HR |
| **Pulse oximeter** with neonatal probe | SpO₂ (right hand and a foot) |
| **Ophthalmoscope** | Red reflex |
| **Pen torch and tongue depressor** (or clean gloved finger) | Palate, mouth |
| **Growth chart** (WHO, INTERGROWTH-21st, Fenton) and **New Ballard chart** | Plotting and gestation |
| **Child Health Card, mother's passport, newborn record** | Documentation, immunisation |
| **Glucometer** (for at-risk babies) | Hypoglycaemia screening |

### First 5 minutes (at birth or on receiving a baby)

1. **Breathing or crying?** If not, start HBB resuscitation (see Neonatal Resuscitation).
2. **Dry, warm, skin-to-skin**, cover the head.
3. **Quick look**: colour, tone, obvious malformation (cleft, NTD, abdominal wall defect, limb anomaly, ambiguous genitalia).
4. **Danger signs** present? → act (oxygen, glucose, antibiotics, SCBU).
5. **Breastfeed within 1 hour**.
6. **Vitamin K 1 mg IM** (0.5 mg preterm or LBW per local protocol), **tetracycline eye ointment**, **chlorhexidine** to the cord, identification band.

### Read the records

Maternal age, parity, ANC findings, HIV (last viral load), syphilis, HBsAg, blood group, gestation by dates or scan, labour events (ROM, fever, meconium, mode of delivery), resuscitation, Apgar, birth weight, drugs given (vitamin K, eye ointment, NVP).

### Focused history (what to find out before you touch the baby)

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Gestation (LNMP, early scan)** | Expected size and maturity; compare with Ballard |
| **Maternal illness**: diabetes, hypertension, HIV (viral load), syphilis, HBsAg, TB, fever | Hypoglycaemia, SGA, infection risk, prophylaxis |
| **Drugs in pregnancy or labour**: anticonvulsants, opioids, MgSO₄ | Malformations, depressed baby |
| **Liquor volume**: oligohydramnios (renal anomalies, DDH, lung hypoplasia), polyhydramnios (oesophageal atresia, GI obstruction) | Directs the examination |
| **Labour**: ROM duration, maternal fever, meconium, breech, instrumental delivery, shoulder dystocia | Sepsis risk, DDH, birth injury |
| **Resuscitation and Apgar** | HIE risk |
| **Family history**: DDH, cleft, heart disease, deafness, sickle cell, early childhood eye problems | Targeted screening |
| **Feeding since birth, urine and meconium passed** | Obstruction, feeding problems |
| **Mother's concerns** | Mothers often notice the problem first |

## 5. Procedure Step by Step

The sequence follows Hutchison's Clinical Methods (24th edition) adapted to the newborn: observe first, measure, then a systematic head-to-toe examination, keeping the baby warm and leaving the parts that make babies cry (hips, Moro, palate palpation) until last.

### A. Introduction, consent and preparation

1. **Introduce yourself**, confirm the baby's identity (name band), explain the examination and get the mother's **consent**; ask the mother about **feeding, urine, stool, and any concerns**.
2. **Wash hands**; warm your hands and stethoscope; warm room, no draught.
3. **Position and exposure**: on a warm, firm surface or in the mother's arms; undress one part at a time, keeping the rest covered.

### B. General inspection (hands off)

1. **Observe the baby quietly** in the mother's arms or cot before undressing.
2. **Colour**: pink, pale, **central cyanosis** (tongue, lips), peripheral acrocyanosis (normal in the first 24–48 h), **jaundice** (in daylight; blanch the skin), plethora.
3. **Breathing**: count **RR for 60 seconds**; look for **grunting, flaring, chest indrawing**, apnoea.
4. **Posture and movement**: a term baby lies **flexed** with symmetrical spontaneous movements; note a limp or "frog-leg" posture (hypotonia), asymmetry (palsy, fracture), jitteriness, seizures.
5. **Cry**: normal, weak, high-pitched (CNS), hoarse (hypothyroidism, laryngeal).
6. **Dysmorphic features**: overall appearance (for example Down syndrome: upslanting palpebral fissures, flat facial profile, epicanthic folds, single palmar crease, sandal gap, hypotonia).

### C. Vital signs and measurements

1. **HR**, **temperature** (axillary, 3–5 min or digital beep), **SpO₂** if indicated (step H).
2. **Weigh** naked on a zeroed scale; record to the nearest 10–20 g.
3. **Head circumference**: tape over the occiput and just above the eyebrows; take the largest of three.
4. **Length**: two people, baby supine on a length board, legs extended.
5. **Plot** all three on the appropriate chart; classify **SGA, AGA or LGA**; note microcephaly or macrocephaly.

### D. Hands

- Count **fingers**; **palmar creases** (single crease: Down syndrome); **polydactyly** (postaxial extra digit on the little-finger side is common and often familial in African babies), syndactyly; nail and peripheral colour.

### E. Head

- Shape, **moulding**, **caput**, **cephalhaematoma**, **subgaleal swelling**; palpate the **sutures** (overriding, widely open, craniosynostosis ridge) and **fontanelles** (size, tension: flat, bulging, sunken).

### F. Face, eyes, ears, nose and mouth

1. **Face**: symmetry at rest and crying (**facial palsy**), dysmorphism.
2. **Eyes**: they open spontaneously when the baby is held upright and gently rocked; look for discharge, swelling, subconjunctival haemorrhage (benign), coloboma, cloudy cornea.
3. **Red reflex**: dim the room; set the ophthalmoscope to **0** dioptres; stand **about 30–50 cm** away; shine the light into both pupils at once (and each eye separately). Normal = a **symmetrical red or orange-red glow** in both pupils (it can look darker or orange in babies with more pigmented fundi: compare the two sides).
4. **Ears**: position, shape, **pre-auricular tags or pits** (consider hearing screening; renal anomalies if with other features).
5. **Nose**: patency of each nostril (watch misting on a cold surface or listen; choanal atresia → cyanosis at rest that improves with crying).
6. **Mouth**: **inspect the palate with a torch and tongue depressor and palpate with a clean gloved finger** (assessing suck at the same time); cleft lip or palate, **Epstein pearls** (benign), natal teeth, tongue-tie, thrush, micrognathia (Pierre Robin). If the baby is calm, the palpation and suck test can wait until the end with the hips and Moro.

### G. Neck and clavicles

- Lumps (cystic hygroma, sternomastoid tumour, goitre), webbing, skin folds; **clavicles** (run fingers along: crepitus, step, swelling = fracture).

### H. Chest and heart

1. **Chest inspection**: shape, symmetry, nipples (supernumerary; breast engorgement is normal); **RR, recession**.
2. **Palpation**: apex position (right-sided in dextrocardia or pneumothorax).
3. **Auscultation** while the baby is quiet: both lung fields (air entry, crackles); **HR and rhythm**, heart sounds, **murmurs** (many soft systolic murmurs in the first days are innocent or transitional, but any murmur with cyanosis, poor feeding, tachypnoea or weak pulses is serious).
4. **Femoral pulses** (and brachial–femoral comparison).
5. **Pulse oximetry**: right hand and a foot where available.

### I. Abdomen, cord and groins

1. **Inspection**: shape (distended, or scaphoid: diaphragmatic hernia); **umbilical cord** (**2 arteries and 1 vein**; single umbilical artery → look for renal and other anomalies); cord stump (clean, dry, chlorhexidine); hernias (umbilical hernias are common and usually close by 2–5 years).
2. **Palpation**: **liver** (normal edge up to 2 cm below the costal margin), spleen tip, kidneys (balloting), masses.
3. **Groins**: inguinal hernia; femoral pulses (if not already done).

### J. Genitalia and anus

1. **Male**: penis length, **meatus position** (hypospadias, epispadias: **do not circumcise**), foreskin, **both testes in the scrotum** (palpate from the groin down), hydrocele (transilluminates, usually resolves), scrotal swelling.
2. **Female**: labia, clitoris size, vaginal opening; **white discharge or a small withdrawal bleed** is normal (maternal oestrogen). **Ambiguous genitalia** (clitoromegaly, fused labia, impalpable gonads) = possible **congenital adrenal hyperplasia**: do not assign sex, check glucose and electrolytes, urgent senior referral.
3. **Anus**: **patency and position**; ask whether meconium has passed (a fistula can pass meconium yet still be abnormal: look at the position).

### K. Legs and feet

- **Toes**, polydactyly, syndactyly; **talipes** (positional talipes corrects fully with gentle passive movement; structural club foot does not → refer for Ponseti casting); limb length; movement of each limb; **brachial plexus palsy** (waiter's tip arm); fractures.

### L. Back and spine

- Turn the baby prone, supported over your hand (ventral suspension also tests tone); inspect the whole spine for **dimples** (especially above the natal cleft or deep with no visible base), **hair tufts, haemangiomas, lipomas, sinuses**, scoliosis, **open neural tube defects** (myelomeningocele: cover with sterile saline-soaked gauze, nurse prone, refer).

### M. Skin

- Jaundice extent, pallor, bruising, petechiae, rashes, pustules; **birthmarks** (Mongolian blue spots: grey-blue patches over the sacrum and buttocks, benign, document them), stork marks, port-wine stain, café-au-lait macules (6 or more = neurofibromatosis), haemangioma (usually appears after birth), **erythema toxicum** (blotchy red with yellow-white centres, day 1–3, baby well), **milia**, **miliaria** (heat rash), peeling (post-term).

### N. Neurological examination and primitive reflexes

1. **Alertness and state**: alert, sleepy, irritable, lethargic.
2. **Tone**: posture; **pull-to-sit** (head lag: term babies show some head lag but should not be floppy); **ventral suspension**; scarf sign.
3. **Primitive reflexes** (do Moro last because it makes the baby cry):

| Reflex | How to elicit | Normal response | Disappears by | Abnormal if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rooting** | Stroke the cheek near the mouth | Turns head toward the stimulus, opens mouth | About **4 months** | Absent: CNS depression, prematurity |
| **Sucking** | Clean finger on the palate | Strong rhythmic suck | About **4 months** (becomes voluntary) | Weak or absent: prematurity, sepsis, HIE, tetanus (trismus) |
| **Palmar grasp** | Press a finger into the palm | Fingers flex and grip | **3–6 months** | Absent in one hand: Klumpke palsy; persistent: CP |
| **Plantar grasp** | Press the sole below the toes | Toes curl | **9–12 months** | Absent: spinal lesion |
| **Stepping / placing** | Hold upright with soles touching the surface | Stepping movements | About **2 months** | |
| **Asymmetric tonic neck reflex (ATNR)** | Turn the head to one side (supine) | "Fencing" posture: arm and leg extend on the face side, flex on the occiput side | About **6 months** | Obligatory or persistent: CP |
| **Galant (trunk incurvation)** | Stroke along one side of the spine (prone) | Trunk curves toward the stroked side | About **4–6 months** | Absent: spinal cord lesion |
| **Moro (startle)** | Support head and back, let the head drop about 1–2 cm suddenly (or pull slightly off the bed and release) | Arms **abduct and extend**, hands open, then **adduct** (embrace) with a cry | About **4–6 months** | **Asymmetric**: **Erb's palsy, clavicle or humerus fracture**; **absent**: severe CNS depression, HIE; **persistent**: CP |

### O. Hips (DDH), last

1. Baby **supine, relaxed** (after a feed is ideal), on a firm surface, nappy removed; examine **one hip at a time**, stabilising the pelvis with the other hand.
2. **Barlow test (provocation)**: hip and knee flexed to 90°, hip **adducted**; apply **gentle downward (posterior) pressure** through the knee. A palpable "clunk" as the femoral head **slips out** of the acetabulum = **dislocatable hip**.
3. **Ortolani test (relocation)**: from the same position, **abduct** the hip while **lifting the greater trochanter forward** with the fingers. A "clunk" as the femoral head **slides back in** = **dislocated, reducible hip**.
4. Look also for **limited abduction**, asymmetric thigh or gluteal skin folds, **leg-length discrepancy** (Galeazzi sign: knees at different heights with hips and knees flexed). Soft "clicks" without movement are usually ligamentous and not significant.
5. **Risk factors** for DDH: **breech** presentation (especially after 36 weeks), **family history** in a first-degree relative, **female**, **first-born**, **oligohydramnios**, large baby, **tight swaddling with hips extended** (a traditional practice in some communities).

### P. Gestational age (New Ballard score), when gestation is uncertain

1. Score **6 neuromuscular signs**: posture, square window (wrist), arm recoil, popliteal angle, scarf sign, heel-to-ear.
2. Score **6 physical signs**: skin, lanugo, plantar surface (creases), breast, eye and ear, genitals.
3. Each sign scores **−1 to 5**; total **−10 to 50**. **Gestation (weeks) = (2 × score + 120) ÷ 5.**

| Total score | Weeks |
|---|---|
| −10 | 20 |
| 0 | 24 |
| 10 | 28 |
| 20 | 32 |
| 25 | 34 |
| 30 | 36 |
| 35 | 38 |
| 40 | 40 |
| 45 | 42 |
| 50 | 44 |

Best done within **12–20 hours** (valid to about 2 weeks; neuromuscular items are unreliable in sick or sedated babies). Accuracy about ± 2 weeks.

### Q. Finishing

1. Dress and wrap the baby, return to the mother (skin-to-skin), **observe a breastfeed** (attachment, effective suckling: UCG).
2. **Wash hands**; **thank** the mother.
3. **Explain findings** to the mother in plain words; address concerns.
4. **Document** everything (including normal findings and benign birthmarks) and plan follow-up.
5. **Check routine care**: vitamin K, tetracycline eye ointment, chlorhexidine cord care, **BCG and OPV0** before discharge (hepatitis B birth dose where introduced) [VERIFY: whether the hepatitis B birth dose is now in the national UNEPI schedule], HIV prophylaxis (NVP, or AZT + NVP) for HIV-exposed babies, risk-based antibiotics, **danger-sign counselling** and the **3–7-day** review.

## 6. Findings and Interpretation

### Screening findings

| Test | Normal | Abnormal and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Red reflex** | Symmetrical red or orange-red glow | **Absent or dull reflex, white pupil (leukocoria), dark spots, or asymmetry** → **cataract, retinoblastoma**, glaucoma, vitreous haemorrhage, retinal detachment → **urgent ophthalmology referral** (UK NIPE: seen within 2 weeks; leukocoria the same week) |
| **Barlow** | No movement | Clunk as the head slips out = dislocatable hip |
| **Ortolani** | No movement | Clunk as the head slides back in = dislocated, reducible hip |
| **Hip clicks** | Soft ligamentous clicks are common | Not significant on their own; limited abduction is |
| **Femoral pulses** | Easily felt, equal to brachial | Weak or absent = **coarctation** or another duct-dependent (left-sided obstructive) lesion |
| **Pulse oximetry** | **Pass**: 95% or more in the right hand or foot with a difference of 3% or less | **Fail**: below 90%; 90–94% or difference above 3% → repeat once, then echo or senior review |
| **Palate** | Intact on inspection **and** palpation | Cleft; a posterior or submucous cleft is missed if you only look |
| **Nostrils** | Patent | Choanal atresia: cyanosis at rest that improves with crying |
| **Anus** | Patent, normal position | Imperforate or anteriorly placed (fistula can still pass meconium) |
| **Meconium** | Within 24–48 h | Over 48 h → Hirschsprung disease, anorectal malformation, meconium ileus |
| **Moro** | Symmetrical | Asymmetric = Erb's palsy, clavicle or humerus fracture; absent = severe CNS depression or HIE |
| **Fontanelle** | Soft, flat | Bulging = raised pressure (meningitis, hydrocephalus); sunken = dehydration |
| **Single umbilical artery** | 2 arteries, 1 vein | Look for renal and other anomalies |

### Preterm versus term features

| Feature | Preterm (about 32 weeks) | Term |
|---|---|---|
| Skin | Thin, red, smooth, visible veins | Thick, pale, cracking or peeling, few vessels |
| Lanugo | Abundant | Thinning, bald areas |
| Plantar creases | Faint red marks, anterior transverse crease only | Creases over the whole sole |
| Breast | Flat areola, no bud or 1–2 mm bud | Full areola, 5–10 mm bud |
| Ear | Soft, slow recoil | Formed, firm, instant recoil |
| Genitals (male) | Testes in upper canal, few rugae | Testes down, good rugae |
| Genitals (female) | Prominent clitoris, small labia minora | Labia majora cover minora and clitoris |
| Posture | Arms and legs more extended | Fully flexed |
| Square window | 45–60° | 0° |
| Scarf sign | Elbow passes midline | Elbow does not reach midline |

### Benign findings: reassure and document

| Finding | Explanation to the mother |
|---|---|
| **Mongolian blue spots** | Normal pigment, very common in African babies, fade over years; **record them** |
| **Erythema toxicum** | Harmless rash in the first days; comes and goes |
| **Milia** | Tiny white spots on the nose; disappear |
| **Epstein pearls** | White cysts on the palate; disappear |
| **Breast engorgement, "witch's milk"** | Mother's hormones; **do not squeeze** (abscess risk) |
| **Vaginal discharge or small bleed** | Mother's hormones; settles in days |
| **Subconjunctival haemorrhage** | From birth pressure; clears in 1–2 weeks |
| **Caput, moulding** | Settle in 1–3 days |
| **Umbilical hernia** | Common; usually closes by 2–5 years; no coins or binders |
| **Acrocyanosis** | Blue hands and feet in the first days; keep warm |
| **Hydrocele** | Fluid around the testis; usually resolves by 1 year |
| **Postaxial polydactyly** | Often familial and isolated |

### Bedside tests

| Test | Indication | Normal / action |
|---|---|---|
| **Glucose** | At risk (preterm, SGA, LGA or infant of a diabetic mother, asphyxia, sepsis, hypothermia, poor feeding) | Treat below **2.6 mmol/L** |
| **Pulse oximetry** | All babies where available (CCHD screening), and any with cyanosis or distress | See screening table above |
| **Transcutaneous bilirubin** | Visible jaundice after 24 h | Plot; serum if high |
| **Orogastric tube** | Polyhydramnios, frothy secretions, choking | Tube coils in the oesophagus (on X-ray) = oesophageal atresia |
| **Transillumination** | Scrotal swelling, suspected pneumothorax | Hydrocele transilluminates |

### Laboratory and imaging

| Test | When | Expected or abnormal finding | How it changes management | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hip ultrasound** | Positive Barlow or Ortolani, or risk factors (breech, family history) | Dysplasia or dislocation (Graf classification) | Pavlik harness; orthopaedic follow-up | Regional or national referral, private |
| **Echocardiography** | Murmur with symptoms, failed oximetry, weak femorals, Down syndrome | Structural heart disease | Prostaglandin or surgery referral | National referral (Uganda Heart Institute), some regional |
| **Renal ultrasound** | Single umbilical artery with other anomalies, ear tags with other features, abdominal mass | Renal anomalies | Urology or nephrology | Most hospitals |
| **Spinal ultrasound** | Atypical sacral dimple, tuft, haemangioma | Tethered cord | Neurosurgery (CURE Hospital Mbale, Mulago) | Referral hospitals |
| **Karyotype, genetics** | Dysmorphism (for example Down syndrome), ambiguous genitalia | Trisomy 21, DSD | Counselling, CAH management | Limited (Kampala, private) |
| **Glucose, electrolytes, 17-OH-progesterone** | Ambiguous genitalia | Salt-wasting CAH | Hydrocortisone, fluids | Limited |
| **Hearing screen (OAE)** | Risk factors (meningitis, severe jaundice, gentamicin, ear anomalies, family history) | Refer result | Audiology | Few centres |
| **Thyroid function** | Prolonged jaundice, Down syndrome, features of hypothyroidism | Low T4, high TSH | Levothyroxine | Referral hospitals |

## 7. Clinical Acumen

### What matters most

Danger signs, vital signs and measurements first; then the "**easy-to-miss five**": **palate**, **red reflex**, **hips**, **femoral pulses**, **anus and spine**.

### Red flags

> [!REDFLAG]
> - **Any IMNCI danger sign**: not feeding, convulsions, RR 60 or more or below 30, grunting, severe indrawing, central cyanosis, temperature above 37.5 °C or below 35.5 °C, floppy or no movement.
> - **Jaundice in the first 24 h**, or yellow palms and soles.
> - **Absent or white red reflex**.
> - **Absent femoral pulses**, SpO₂ below 95% or a difference above 3% between hand and foot, cyanosis, a loud or harsh murmur.
> - **Bilious vomiting**, abdominal distension, **no meconium by 48 h**, imperforate anus.
> - **Bulging or very large fontanelle**, OFC above +2 SD or below −2 SD.
> - **Ambiguous genitalia** (possible salt-losing CAH).
> - **Open neural tube defect**, gastroschisis or omphalocele.
> - **Excessive drooling and choking with feeds** (oesophageal atresia: pass an orogastric tube).
> - **Weight loss above 10%**, or a mother worried that the baby "is not right".

### Thinking like a clinician

- **A "normal" examination in a baby who is not feeding is not normal.** Feeding problems are the commonest early sign of sepsis, hypoglycaemia, HIE and malformations.
- **Use the history to target the examination**: polyhydramnios → pass a tube to exclude oesophageal atresia; breech → careful hip examination; diabetic mother → glucose, heart, sacrum (caudal regression); instrumental delivery → scalp and facial nerve.
- **Peripheral cyanosis (hands and feet) is normal in the first days; central cyanosis (tongue) is never normal.**
- **Distinguish benign from serious skin findings**: Mongolian spots versus bruises (spots are present at birth, uniform, non-tender, do not change quickly); erythema toxicum versus pustular infection (well baby, sterile centres, changes location).
- **One anomaly → look for more** (single umbilical artery → kidneys; ear tags → hearing and kidneys; Down syndrome features → heart (AVSD), duodenal atresia, hypothyroidism).
- **Re-examine at 6 weeks**: hips and testes can change, cataracts may become visible, murmurs appear as pulmonary pressure falls.
- **Listen to the mother**: dismissing her concern gives false reassurance.

### Common mistakes (missed diagnoses)

- Posterior cleft palate (looked but did not feel).
- Cataract or retinoblastoma (no red reflex check).
- Coarctation (femorals not felt).
- Imperforate anus (not inspected; "he passed meconium").
- DDH (hips not examined).
- Sepsis (danger signs not checked).
- Mongolian spots not documented, later mislabelled as bruises.

### When to stop and escalate

- Stop and stabilise if the baby is cold, apnoeic, cyanosed or convulses.
- Senior review the same day for any red flag; urgent referral for absent red reflex, ambiguous genitalia, failed oximetry or absent femorals, imperforate anus, open NTD or abdominal wall defect.

### Worked example (Ballard)

> [!EXAM]
> A baby whose LNMP is unknown scores **25** → (2 × 25 + 120) ÷ 5 = **34 weeks**. If she weighs 1.6 kg, plot at 34 weeks: about the 10th centile → borderline SGA; she needs KMC, feeding support and glucose monitoring.

## 8. Complications and Troubleshooting

### Risks of the examination

The examination itself is safe if done gently; risks and pitfalls include:

- **Hypothermia** from a long examination with the baby uncovered.
- **Hip injury** from forceful or repeated Barlow or Ortolani.
- **Infection** from unwashed hands or equipment.
- **Missed diagnoses** (section 7).
- **False reassurance** when a mother's concern is dismissed.
- **Mislabelling** of Mongolian spots as bruises (child-protection harm) when not documented.

### Troubleshooting

- **Crying baby**: let the mother settle or feed the baby; do the "quiet" parts (observation, auscultation, red reflex) when calm; leave hips, Moro and palate palpation to last.
- **Eyes closed for the red reflex**: hold the baby upright and rock gently; dim the room.
- **Cold baby**: stop, rewarm skin-to-skin, finish later.

### Managing abnormal findings

| Finding | Likely meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Any **danger sign** | Sepsis, hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, HIE, CHD | Stabilise; **ampicillin + gentamicin**; glucose; warmth; SCBU or referral |
| **Jaundice day 1**, palms and soles yellow | Haemolysis, sepsis | **TSB urgently**, phototherapy per thresholds |
| **Weight below 2.5 kg** | LBW | KMC, feeding support; **below 1.5 kg or below 32 weeks: refer** (UCG) |
| **Absent or abnormal red reflex** | Cataract, retinoblastoma | **Urgent ophthalmology referral** (for example Mulago, Ruharo, Mengo eye units) |
| **Positive Barlow or Ortolani**, limited abduction | DDH | Ultrasound and **orthopaedic referral** (CoRSU, Mulago, regional orthopaedics); avoid tight swaddling; Pavlik harness if confirmed |
| **Breech or family history, normal examination** | DDH risk | Hip ultrasound at about 6 weeks (where available) and careful 6-week examination |
| **Club foot (structural)** | Congenital talipes equinovarus | Refer early for **Ponseti casting** (clubfoot clinics at CoRSU, Mulago and many regional referral hospitals) |
| **Positional talipes** | Intrauterine moulding | Reassure; passive stretching |
| **Cleft lip or palate** | Feeding problem | Support breastfeeding or **cup or spoon EBM** (UCG); refer to cleft surgery (CoRSU, Mulago); check for other anomalies |
| **Absent femoral pulses, failed oximetry, cyanosis** | Duct-dependent CHD, coarctation | Senior review, oxygen cautiously, **echo**, refer (Uganda Heart Institute) |
| **Imperforate anus, no meconium by 48 h, bilious vomiting** | Anorectal malformation, Hirschsprung, malrotation | **Nil by mouth**, NGT on free drainage, IV fluids, urgent surgical referral |
| **Undescended testis** | Cryptorchidism | Recheck at 6–8 weeks; refer for orchidopexy by **6–12 months** if not descended (bilateral impalpable with hypospadias = DSD, urgent) |
| **Hypospadias** | Urethral meatus on the underside | **Do not circumcise**; refer to paediatric surgery |
| **Ambiguous genitalia** | CAH, DSD | Do not assign sex; glucose, electrolytes; urgent referral |
| **Open neural tube defect** | Myelomeningocele | Cover with sterile saline-soaked gauze, nurse prone, avoid latex, **refer** (CURE Children's Hospital Mbale); folic acid counselling for the next pregnancy |
| **Sacral dimple** (deep, above the natal cleft, with tuft or haemangioma) | Occult spinal dysraphism | Spinal ultrasound; neurosurgery |
| **Gastroschisis or omphalocele** | Abdominal wall defect | Cover with cling film or a sterile bag, keep warm, NGT, IV fluids, urgent surgical referral |
| **Down syndrome features** | Trisomy 21 | Sensitive disclosure with both parents; echo; thyroid; feeding support; refer |
| **Asymmetric Moro, limp arm** | Erb's palsy, clavicle fracture | See Common Neonatal Disorders (birth injuries) |
| **Large tense fontanelle, fast-growing OFC** | Hydrocephalus, meningitis | Senior review; cranial ultrasound; LP if infection suspected |
| **Polydactyly (postaxial, narrow stalk)** | Often familial, isolated | Refer for removal; avoid tying off at home with thread unless your unit's protocol supports it |
| **Natal teeth (loose)** | Aspiration risk | Dental review |
| **Tongue-tie with feeding problems** | Ankyloglossia | Assess feeding; frenotomy if feeding affected |

### Discharge checklist (UCG)

- **At least 24 h old**, feeding well (8 or more feeds in 24 h), temperature normal, no danger signs.
- Do **not** plan early discharge for **small (LBW or preterm)** babies or babies not feeding well.
- **BCG and OPV0** given; HIV prophylaxis started if exposed; vitamin K and eye ointment done.
- Counsel on **danger signs** (refusal to feed, excessive crying, cord bleeding, fever, bulging fontanelle, abdominal distension, grunting, yellow palms and soles), **cord care**, **warmth**, **exclusive breastfeeding**, **sleeping under a treated net**.
- **Next visit at 3–7 days**; immunisation at **6 weeks**.

> [!UGANDA]
> Many Ugandan facilities discharge mothers within hours of a normal birth because of bed pressure. UCG 2023 says **do not discharge a baby under 24 hours old**; if a mother insists on leaving early, do the full examination before she goes, give BCG and OPV0, counsel on danger signs and book the 3–7-day review.

### Doses used in this topic

| Drug | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin K (phytomenadione) | **1 mg IM** (0.5 mg preterm or LBW, per local protocol) | At birth |
| Tetracycline eye ointment | Both eyes once | At birth |
| Chlorhexidine (7.1% chlorhexidine digluconate) | To the cord stump | Cord care |
| Nevirapine | **15 mg** once daily (birth weight ≥ 2.5 kg) | Low-risk HIV-exposed infant: daily for 6 weeks [VERIFY: weight-band doses and duration against the current Uganda HIV guidelines] |
| 10% dextrose | **2 mL/kg** IV | Glucose below 2.6 mmol/L |

## 9. Documentation and Presenting Findings

### Communication and documentation

- Use plain, honest language; deliver unexpected findings (for example Down syndrome, cleft) **with both parents present** where possible, in private, with a senior.
- Do not blame the mother; explain the next steps and referral clearly; write a referral letter.
- Document **measurements with centiles**, **all systems** including normal findings, and **benign marks**; record vaccines and prophylaxis given.
- **State normal findings explicitly** ("palate intact on inspection and palpation; red reflexes present bilaterally").

### Model summary line (normal baby)

> "A **20-hour-old** term (39 weeks by early scan; Ballard 38) male, birth weight **3.25 kg (AGA)**, OFC 34.5 cm, length 50 cm, born by SVD after an uncomplicated labour, mother HIV-negative, RPR negative, HBsAg negative. He is breastfeeding 8 times in 24 h with good attachment, and has passed urine and meconium. Examination is normal: RR 44, HR 136, T 36.9 °C, SpO₂ 98% right hand and 97% foot, palate intact, red reflexes present and symmetrical, hips stable, femoral pulses normal, anus patent, spine intact, Mongolian spots over the sacrum documented. Vitamin K, eye ointment, chlorhexidine, BCG and OPV0 given. Plan: discharge after 24 h with danger-sign counselling and 3–7-day review."

### Model case presentation (ward round)

> "**Baby boy of Nabirye**, **18 hours old**, born at **38 weeks** by early scan (Ballard 36–38) by SVD after a 9-hour labour, ROM 4 hours, clear liquor, **Apgar 8 and 10**; no resuscitation. Birth weight **3.1 kg (50th centile, AGA)**, OFC **34 cm (25th centile)**, length **49 cm**.
>
> Mother is a 24-year-old para 2, **HIV-positive on TLD with a suppressed viral load (below 50 copies) at 34 weeks**, RPR negative, HBsAg negative, blood group O positive. The baby is **low-risk HIV-exposed** and received **NVP 15 mg** at 2 hours; plan **NVP daily for 6 weeks** and **DNA PCR at 6 weeks**. He is **exclusively breastfeeding**, 7 feeds so far, good attachment; passed urine and meconium.
>
> On examination he is pink, active, with a normal cry; RR 46, HR 140, T 36.8 °C, SpO₂ 98% hand and 97% foot. Anterior fontanelle 2 × 2 cm, flat. Palate intact on inspection and palpation. **Red reflexes present and symmetrical.** Heart sounds normal, no murmur, **femoral pulses normal**. Abdomen soft, 3 cord vessels, stump clean with chlorhexidine. Both testes descended, meatus normal, anus patent. **Hips stable** on Barlow and Ortolani. Spine intact. **Mongolian spots** over the sacrum, documented. Normal tone; rooting, suck, grasp and symmetrical Moro present.
>
> **Vitamin K, tetracycline eye ointment, BCG and OPV0** given. **Assessment:** healthy term AGA HIV-exposed infant. **Plan:** discharge after 24 h, counsel on danger signs and exclusive breastfeeding, NVP adherence, review at 3–7 days, 6-week immunisation and DNA PCR."

## 11. Key Points

### High-yield points

- **Observe before you touch; keep the baby warm**; hips, Moro and palate palpation last.
- Examine **at birth, within 24 h and before discharge, at 3–7 days and at 6 weeks**; no discharge under 24 h (UCG).
- **Measure and plot** weight, OFC and length; classify SGA, AGA or LGA.
- **Danger signs first**: a baby who is not feeding is not well.
- The **easy-to-miss five**: palate (look and feel), red reflex, hips, femoral pulses, anus and spine.
- **Absent or white red reflex = urgent referral** (retinoblastoma, cataract).
- **Barlow = out, Ortolani = back in**; clicks are not clunks.
- **Ballard: (2 × score + 120) ÷ 5.**
- **Central cyanosis is never normal**; acrocyanosis is.
- **Document Mongolian spots.**

|---|---|
| Normal term weight | **2.5–4.0 kg** |
| Normal OFC | About **33–37 cm** (mean about 35) |
| Normal length | About **48–53 cm** |
| RR / HR | **30–60** / **100–160** per minute |
| Temperature | **36.5–37.5 °C** axillary |
| SpO₂ screening | Pass **95% or more** with a hand–foot difference **3% or less**; fail **below 90%** |
| Weight loss | Up to **7–10%**; regain by **10–14 days** |
| Urine / meconium | Within **24 h** / within **24–48 h** |
| Anterior fontanelle | **1–4 cm**; closes by **12–18 months** |
| Posterior fontanelle closure | **6–8 weeks** |
| Ballard formula | **(2 × score + 120) ÷ 5**; best at **12–20 h**, valid to about 2 weeks |
| Moro disappears | **4–6 months** |
| Palmar / plantar grasp | **3–6 months** / **9–12 months** |
| Glucose treatment threshold | Below **2.6 mmol/L** |

| Refer LBW | Below **1.5 kg** or below **32 weeks** (UCG) |
| Undescended testis | Refer for surgery by **6–12 months** |

### Exam traps

- **"Palate looks fine on inspection, so it is intact."** → You must also **palpate** (and see the back with a torch): posterior and submucous clefts are missed.
- **"Bluish patches on the buttocks: suspect abuse."** → Usually **Mongolian spots**: present at birth, benign; **document** them.
- **"Blue hands and feet at 6 hours: cyanotic heart disease."** → **Acrocyanosis** is normal; check the **tongue** for central cyanosis and do oximetry.
- **"A click on hip examination = DDH."** → Soft ligamentous clicks are common; the significant finding is a **clunk** of dislocation or relocation, or limited abduction.
- **"Absent red reflex can wait for the 6-week check."** → Wrong. **Refer urgently** (retinoblastoma, cataract).
- **"He passed meconium, so the anus is normal."** → A fistula can pass meconium; **inspect the anal position and patency**.
- **"Circumcise to correct hypospadias."** → Never circumcise: the foreskin is needed for repair.
- **"Squeeze the swollen breasts to remove the milk."** → Wrong: causes mastitis or abscess.

### Questions seniors ask

**Viva questions**

1. *What is the normal RR and HR of a newborn?* RR 30–60/min; HR 100–160/min.
2. *How do you perform the Ortolani test, and what does a positive test mean?* Flex hip and knee to 90°, abduct while lifting the greater trochanter; a clunk as the head relocates = a dislocated but reducible hip.
3. *Why examine the red reflex?* To detect cataract and retinoblastoma early; an absent or white reflex needs urgent referral.
4. *When does the Moro reflex disappear and what does an asymmetric Moro suggest?* About 4–6 months; brachial plexus palsy or clavicle or humerus fracture.
5. *How do you calculate gestation from the New Ballard score?* (2 × score + 120) ÷ 5.
6. *Why feel the femoral pulses?* To detect coarctation of the aorta or other left-sided obstructive lesions.
7. *Name five benign findings in a newborn.* Mongolian spots, erythema toxicum, milia, Epstein pearls, breast engorgement (also vaginal discharge, subconjunctival haemorrhage, umbilical hernia).

**On the ward round**

1. **"Is the baby the right size for gestation?"** Give weight, OFC and length with centiles and the Ballard estimate.
2. **"Did you check the palate, red reflex, hips, femorals and anus?"** Say yes, and how.
3. **"What is the mother's HIV status and what prophylaxis has the baby received?"** Viral load, risk category, NVP ± AZT dose, PCR plan.
4. **"Has the baby fed, passed urine and meconium?"** Times and number of feeds.
5. **"What benign findings did you note?"** For example Mongolian spots, erythema toxicum, documented.
6. **"Can this baby go home today?"** Over 24 h, feeding well, normal examination, vaccines and prophylaxis given, mother counselled, follow-up booked.
7. **"What would make you refer?"** Danger signs, absent red reflex, unstable hip, absent femorals or failed oximetry, imperforate anus, ambiguous genitalia, NTD, cleft with feeding failure.

### Mnemonics

- **Order of the examination: "Look, Measure, Head-to-Toe, Turn, Test"**: look (colour, breathing, posture), measure (weight, OFC, length, temperature), head-to-toe (supine), turn (back and spine), test (reflexes, hips last).
- **Easy-to-miss checks: "Please Remember Five Hidden Abnormalities, Seriously"**: **P**alate, **R**ed reflex, **F**emoral pulses, **H**ips, **A**nus, **S**pine.
- **Reflex disappearance: "2-4-6-12"**: stepping by **2** months, rooting and sucking by **4**, Moro and ATNR by about **6**, plantar grasp by **12** months.
- **Barlow versus Ortolani: "Barlow = Bye-bye (out), Ortolani = Only back In"**.

### OSCE tips

- Talk to the mother throughout; keep the baby warm; wash hands at the start and end.
- Do the **hips and Moro last** (they make the baby cry).
- **State normal findings aloud** ("palate intact on inspection and palpation; red reflexes present bilaterally").
- End with a **one-line summary and plan**.
