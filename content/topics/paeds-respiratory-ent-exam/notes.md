## 1. Purpose and Indications

The respiratory and ENT examination of a child is the **most frequently performed examination on the Ugandan paediatric ward**: pneumonia, malaria with fast breathing, asthma, bronchiolitis, TB, croup and ear infections fill the beds and the OPD. The examination has two goals:

1. **Recognise the sick child fast**: count the breaths, look for indrawing and danger signs, measure SpO₂, and decide whether the child needs oxygen and admission **before** you finish the detailed examination.
2. **Find the cause**: localise the problem (upper airway, lower airway, lung parenchyma, pleura, outside the lung) and examine the ears, nose and throat, which are often the source of fever.

A child is not a small adult: **most of the examination is observation**, done while the child sits on the parent's lap. Auscultation in a crying infant is unreliable; counting, looking and measuring are reliable. That is why WHO/IMCI built pneumonia classification on **respiratory rate, chest indrawing, danger signs and SpO₂**, not on crackles.

> [!PEARL]
> The one thing never to forget: **look before you touch, count before you listen, and leave the throat until last.** A calm child gives you an accurate RR and a readable SpO₂; an upset child gives you neither, and in a child with upper-airway obstruction, upsetting them can be fatal.

**Indications**

- Every child with cough, difficult or fast breathing, noisy breathing, fever, ear pain or discharge, sore throat, neck swelling, poor feeding, or failure to thrive.
- Every admitted child daily (RR, SpO₂, work of breathing), and every child with HIV, TB contact, malnutrition or sickle cell disease at each visit.

## 2. Contraindications and Precautions

- **Do not examine the throat** with a spatula (and avoid lying the child down, IM injections or painful procedures) in a child with **stridor, drooling, tripod posture or toxic appearance**: suspected **epiglottitis**, retropharyngeal abscess or severe croup. Secure the airway first.
- **Do not delay resuscitation** to complete the examination: a child with emergency signs gets oxygen and ABC care first.
- **Otoscopy**: don't insert the speculum blindly or force it in a struggling child; avoid if you suspect a canal **foreign body** that you might push further (refer for removal with proper instruments).
- **Percussion and tactile fremitus** are of limited value and can upset infants: do them in older children or when you suspect an effusion.
- **Infection control**: if TB or pertussis is suspected in an adolescent/adult carer, use masks and ventilation; hand hygiene before and after; clean specula between children.
- **Warmth and privacy**: expose briefly in neonates and small infants; offer privacy and a chaperone to older girls and adolescents.

## 3. Relevant Anatomy and Normal Values

### Normal vital signs by age (APLS, as used on Ugandan wards)

| Age | Respiratory rate (/min) | Heart rate (/min) | Systolic BP (mmHg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Newborn | 40–60 | 110–170 | 60–80 |
| <1 year | 30–40 | 110–160 | 70–90 |
| 1–2 years | 25–35 | 100–150 | 80–95 |
| 2–5 years | 25–30 | 95–140 | 80–100 |
| 5–12 years | 20–25 | 80–120 | 90–110 |
| >12 years | 15–20 | 60–100 | 100–120 |

Minimum acceptable SBP ≈ **70 + (2 × age in years)** for children 1–10 years. **SpO₂ normal ≥95%** at Kampala's altitude (about 1,200 m); **<90% = hypoxaemia** needing oxygen.

### Classification thresholds

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| **WHO fast breathing** | **≥60/min (<2 months)**, **≥50 (2–11 months)**, **≥40 (1–4 years)**, **>30 (≥5 years, UCG 2023)** |
| Severe tachypnoea | RR **≥70** |
| Capillary refill | Normal **≤2 s**; abnormal **>3 s** |
| Apnoea (infant) | Pause **>20 s** (or shorter with bradycardia/cyanosis) |
| Ear discharge | **<14 days** acute; **≥14 days** chronic (CSOM) |
| Cervical nodes | Up to **1 cm** common and normal; **>2 cm**, or any **supraclavicular** node, needs explanation |
| Chest expansion (≥5 years) | Normal **≥3–5 cm** |

### Surface anatomy that matters at the bedside

- **Axillae**: the **right middle lobe and lingula** project there; consolidation can be missed if you listen only at the front and back.
- **Trachea**: central in the suprasternal notch (palpate gently in older children only).
- **Liver dullness**: normally begins at the **5th–6th rib in the mid-clavicular line**; lower = hyperinflation pushing the liver down.
- **Infant chest wall** is compliant: indrawing appears early; infants breathe **diaphragmatically**, so you count the RR by watching the **abdomen**.
- **Ear canal**: in infants the canal is straightened by pulling the pinna **down and back**; in older children **up and back**.
- **Normal tympanic membrane**: **pearly grey, translucent**, cone of light **anteroinferiorly**, handle of the **malleus** visible, mobile on pneumatic otoscopy.
- **Frontal sinuses** develop after about **7 years**; maxillary sinus tenderness is more useful in younger children.
- **Tonsils** sit in the tonsillar fossae between the palatine arches; their size is graded against the oropharyngeal width (Brodsky, section 6).

## 4. Equipment and Preparation

### Equipment

| Item | Use / tip |
|---|---|
| **Watch or respiratory timer** (IMCI timer, phone) | Count RR for 60 s |
| **Pulse oximeter** with neonatal/paediatric/adult probes | Correct probe size; finger, toe, palm or foot in infants |
| **Stethoscope** with a **small (paediatric) diaphragm and bell** | Warm it first; small chest = small diaphragm |
| **Otoscope** with charged batteries and **specula of several sizes** (disposable or cleaned between children) | Largest speculum that fits the canal |
| **Pen torch** and **wooden tongue depressors** | Throat; one depressor per child |
| **Tape measure, weighing scale, length board, MUAC tape** | Growth (Z-scores) |
| **Thermometer** (axillary digital) | Fever |
| **Peak flow meter** with mouthpieces and a height chart | Children ≥5–6 years with asthma |
| **Tuning fork 512 Hz** | Rinne and Weber in older children |
| **Gloves, alcohol hand rub, clean specula** | Infection prevention between children |
| **Toys, stickers or a phone video** | Distraction |

### Preparation, consent, positioning and exposure

1. **Wash your hands** (alcohol rub) and introduce yourself to the carer and child. Confirm the child's name and age. Explain what you will do; ask permission (consent from the carer, **assent** from the child).
2. Note the **age** (it sets every normal value), **weight** and whether the child is **asleep, calm, crying or feeding**.
3. **Position**: **infants and toddlers on the parent's lap**, facing you or sideways; **older children** sitting on the bed, then lying at 45°.
4. **Exposure**: **undress the chest** fully (vest off), keeping the child warm; in older girls and adolescents, offer privacy and a chaperone.
5. **Warm** your hands and stethoscope; use a toy or the parent's voice to distract.
6. **Plan an opportunistic order**: do the quiet things first (observe, count, auscultate if asleep), and the upsetting things last (ears, throat, temperature in some children).

## 5. Procedure Step by Step

The sequence follows **Hutchison's Clinical Methods (24th edition)**: general inspection with vital signs and growth → hands → face → neck → chest (inspection → palpation → percussion → auscultation) → completing the examination. For children it is adapted: **observe first, auscultate whenever the child is quiet** (even if that is out of order), and do the **ears, nose and throat last**.

### Step 1. Introduction, consent and hand hygiene

As in section 4: hand hygiene, introduction, identity, explanation, consent and assent.

### Step 2. Position and exposure

Parent's lap for infants and toddlers; older children sitting, then at 45°; chest exposed, warm; chaperone offered.

### Step 3. General inspection (from the end of the bed)

- **Well or unwell?** Alert, playing, feeding, smiling vs lethargic, irritable, floppy.
- **Posture**: tripod/sniffing (epiglottitis), sitting forward (asthma), lying still (pleuritic pain).
- **Colour**: central cyanosis (tongue), pallor (palms, conjunctivae), jaundice.
- **Audible noises**: stridor, wheeze, grunting, cough (barking = croup; paroxysmal with whoop = pertussis; wet = secretions/bronchiectasis; staccato in young infants = chlamydia).
- **Work of breathing**: nasal flaring, head nodding, tracheal tug, indrawing, abdominal breathing, use of accessory muscles.
- **Ability to talk/feed** (asthma severity, respiratory distress).
- **Drooling, mouth breathing**, voice (hoarse in croup, muffled in epiglottitis/quinsy).
- **Nutritional status**: wasting, oedema, hair changes; measure weight, length/height, MUAC; plot Z-scores.
- **Equipment around the bed**: oxygen, nebuliser, inhalers, sputum pot, NG tube, drains.

### Step 4. Vital signs

1. **Respiratory rate**: child calm, chest exposed; watch the **abdomen** in infants (diaphragmatic breathing); count for **a full 60 seconds**; repeat if borderline or if the child was crying. State the result against the WHO cut-off ("RR 54: fast for a 9-month-old").
2. **Pulse oximetry**: choose the right probe; place on a finger/toe (big toe or foot in infants, wrapped in the neonatal sensor); keep the limb still and warm; wait for a **steady pleth waveform** and a stable reading for **30–60 seconds**; compare the pulse rate with your own count. Note **in air** or on oxygen (and the flow).
3. **Heart rate**, **capillary refill** (press the sternum or fingertip for 5 s; normal ≤2 s; >3 s abnormal), **temperature** (axillary), **BP** (appropriate cuff: bladder covers 80–100% of arm circumference) when unwell.

> [!PEARL]
> **Pulse oximeters can over-read in children with dark skin** (by 2–3% on average) and when perfusion is poor or the child moves. Treat a borderline reading (90–93%) in a child with signs of distress as possible hypoxaemia, repeat it, and watch the child, not only the number.

### Step 5. Hands

**Clubbing** (look along the nail from the side; Schamroth window test in older children), peripheral cyanosis, warmth, CRT, palmar pallor, flapping tremor (CO₂ retention, rare in children), salbutamol tremor.

### Step 6. Face

Central cyanosis (tongue), nasal flaring, conjunctival pallor, **allergic shiners and nasal crease** (atopy), facial puffiness, parotid swelling (HIV), periorbital swelling (sinusitis, nephrotic).

### Step 7. Neck and lymph nodes of the head and neck

1. **Tracheal position** (older children; gently, the trachea should be central in the suprasternal notch), **tracheal tug**, JVP (older children).
2. **Lymph nodes**: stand **behind** an older child (in front for a toddler on the parent's lap), head slightly flexed.
3. Palpate in order: **submental → submandibular → tonsillar (jugulodigastric) → anterior cervical chain → posterior cervical chain → supraclavicular → pre-auricular → post-auricular → occipital**.
4. For each node: **site, size** (cervical nodes up to 1 cm are common and normal in children; >2 cm, or any **supraclavicular** node, needs explanation), **consistency** (soft, rubbery, firm, hard), **tenderness**, **mobility**, **matting**, **fluctuation**, overlying skin (redness, **sinus**).
5. Check the **axillae** (BCG adenitis on the vaccinated side) and **groin**, and feel for **hepatosplenomegaly** if nodes are generalised.

### Step 8. Chest: inspection

1. **Shape**: normal, barrel (hyperinflation), **pectus excavatum/carinatum**, **Harrison sulci**, rickety rosary (rickets), asymmetry (collapse, effusion, scoliosis).
2. **Scars**: chest drain sites, thoracotomy, BCG scar (right upper arm in Uganda).
3. **Movement**: symmetry (reduced on the side of pathology), **lower chest wall indrawing** (state whether it is present all the time in a calm child), intercostal, subcostal, suprasternal recession, paradoxical (see-saw) breathing.
4. **Respiratory pattern**: rate, depth (deep acidotic breathing in malaria/DKA/dehydration), apnoeas (count pauses >20 s in infants), periodic breathing (preterms).

### Step 9. Chest: palpation

1. **Trachea** (older children) and **apex beat** (mediastinal shift: deviated away from effusion/pneumothorax, towards collapse).
2. **Chest expansion** (children ≥5 y): hands around the lower chest, thumbs meeting in the midline, ask for a deep breath; normal ≥3–5 cm; unequal = pathology on the side that moves less.
3. **Tactile vocal fremitus** (older children: "say ninety-nine"; infants: palpate during crying): increased over consolidation, decreased over effusion/pneumothorax.
4. Feel for **subcutaneous emphysema** (crackling under the skin of the neck/chest: pneumothorax, pneumomediastinum in asthma) and **tenderness**.

### Step 10. Chest: percussion

- Use light percussion (one finger directly on the chest in infants; standard technique in older children), comparing **side to side**, front, sides (axillae) and back.
- **Resonant** = normal; **hyper-resonant** = pneumothorax, hyperinflation; **dull** = consolidation, collapse; **stony dull** = **pleural effusion/empyema**.
- Liver dullness normally begins at the 5th–6th rib in the mid-clavicular line; lower = hyperinflation.

### Step 11. Chest: auscultation

Do this **early if the child is quiet** (asleep on the parent), then return to the sequence.

1. Use the **small diaphragm**, warmed; listen when the child is quiet (asleep on the parent, or after distraction); in older children ask them to breathe in and out through the mouth.
2. Listen **symmetrically**: front (upper, middle, lower zones), axillae (essential: the right middle lobe and lingula are heard there), back (upper, lower), comparing left and right at each level.
3. Describe:
   - **Breath sounds**: vesicular (normal) vs **bronchial** (consolidation); **reduced/absent** (effusion, pneumothorax, collapse, silent chest).
   - **Added sounds**: fine/coarse **crackles** (inspiratory, early or late), **wheeze** (expiratory, polyphonic vs monophonic), **stridor** (listen over the neck), **pleural rub**.
   - **Vocal resonance** in older children (increased over consolidation, absent over effusion).
4. **Transmitted upper-airway sounds** are loudest over the trachea/neck, symmetrical, and change after a cough or after clearing the nose: listen over the neck and ask the child to cough (or suction the nose) before calling them crackles or wheeze.
5. Finish by listening to the **heart** (murmurs, gallop in heart failure, muffled sounds in pericardial effusion) and palpating the **liver** (hepatomegaly in heart failure; pushed down in hyperinflation).

### Step 12. Ears

1. **Inspect** the pinna and **behind the ear** (mastoid: redness, swelling, tenderness, pinna pushed forward and down = mastoiditis), pre-/post-auricular nodes, discharge, smell.
2. **Position**: child on the parent's lap, sideways, one of the parent's arms around the child's body and arms, the other hand holding the head against the parent's chest.
3. **Otoscope**: choose the **largest speculum** that fits; hold the otoscope like a pen, **brace the little finger on the child's head** so the otoscope moves with the head.
4. **Straighten the canal**: **infants – pull the pinna gently down and back; older children – up and back.**
5. Look at the **canal** (wax, foreign body, swelling and pain on moving the pinna = otitis externa, discharge) and the **tympanic membrane** (colour, translucency, position, light reflex, malleus, perforation, discharge; table in section 6).
6. **Hearing**: infants – **distraction test** (sound behind and to the side; child turns), parental concern, speech milestones; older children – **whisper test** (masking the other ear), **Rinne** (512 Hz fork: air conduction louder than bone = normal or sensorineural loss; bone > air = conductive loss) and **Weber** (lateralises to the conductive-loss ear).

### Step 13. Nose

1. **Inspect** externally: shape, nasal crease (allergic salute), flaring, discharge (clear, purulent, **unilateral foul** = foreign body; bloody), vestibulitis.
2. **Anterior rhinoscopy**: push the tip up gently with your thumb and look with the otoscope (large speculum) or torch: septum (deviation, haematoma after trauma, perforation), **inferior turbinates** (pale, swollen = allergic rhinitis; red = infection), **polyps** (grey, glistening; think **cystic fibrosis** in a child), foreign body.
3. **Patency**: hold a cold metal spatula or mirror under each nostril (misting), or watch a wisp of cotton wool move; important in neonates (choanal atresia: pass a fine catheter).
4. **Sinuses** (older children): tenderness over maxillary and frontal sinuses (frontal sinuses develop after about 7 years); periorbital swelling.

### Step 14. Mouth and throat (leave until last)

> [!REDFLAG]
> **Stop.** If the child has **stridor, drooling, a muffled voice, tripod posture or looks toxic**, do NOT examine the throat. Say so out loud in an OSCE, call for senior/anaesthetic help, and keep the child calm with the parent.

1. **Position**: child on the parent's lap facing forward; parent's one arm across the child's arms and chest, other hand on the forehead holding the head against the parent's chest.
2. Ask older children to **open wide and say "aah"** (or "show me your lion roar"); many children will open without a spatula.
3. Use a **torch** and, if needed, a **tongue depressor** placed on the **anterior two-thirds of the tongue** (not the back: it triggers gagging); press down briefly.
4. Look at:
   - **Lips and mouth**: cyanosis, angular stomatitis, cheilitis, **oral thrush** (HIV, antibiotics), ulcers (herpes, HFMD), **Koplik spots** (measles: white spots on the buccal mucosa opposite the molars), gums (gingivitis, bleeding), teeth (caries, dental abscess), tongue (strawberry tongue in scarlet fever/Kawasaki).
   - **Palate**: petechiae (GAS, EBV), cleft, bulging soft palate (quinsy).
   - **Tonsils**: size (Brodsky grade), symmetry, redness, **exudate** (white/yellow spots), **membrane** (grey, adherent, bleeds when removed = **diphtheria**), ulcers.
   - **Uvula**: central or **deviated away** from a quinsy.
   - **Posterior pharyngeal wall**: redness, lymphoid follicles, bulging (retropharyngeal abscess), post-nasal drip.

### Step 15. Completing the examination

- **Peak flow** (≥5–6 y with asthma): standing, best of three.
- **Growth**: weight, height/length, MUAC; Z-scores.
- **Other systems** guided by findings: heart (murmurs, gallop), abdomen (liver, spleen), CNS (meningism in a febrile child), skin (rash, eczema).
- **Thank** the child and carer, cover the child, wash hands, clean the otoscope specula.
- **Summarise** findings to the examiner/senior with a **WHO classification** (section 9).

## 6. Findings and Interpretation

### Definitions of the signs

| Sign | Definition | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| **Fast breathing (tachypnoea)** | RR at or above the WHO age cut-off (≥60 / ≥50 / ≥40; >30 at ≥5 y) | Pneumonia, bronchiolitis, asthma; also acidosis (malaria, DKA, dehydration), fever, heart failure |
| **Lower chest wall indrawing** | Lower chest wall moves **in** on **inspiration** (subcostal) | Stiff lungs or airway obstruction: pneumonia (IMCI "pneumonia"), bronchiolitis, asthma |
| **Recession** | Soft tissues between ribs (intercostal), above the sternum (suprasternal/tracheal tug) or clavicles sink on inspiration | Increased work of breathing; suprasternal recession suggests upper-airway obstruction |
| **Nasal flaring** | Widening of the nostrils with inspiration | Respiratory distress; predicts hypoxaemia in infants |
| **Grunting** | Short noise at the end of expiration (partially closed glottis) | Severe distress: pneumonia, neonatal respiratory distress, sepsis |
| **Head nodding (bobbing)** | Head moves with each breath from sternomastoid use (infants) | Severe distress, hypoxaemia |
| **Central cyanosis** | Blue tongue/inner lips | Hypoxaemia (SpO₂ usually <80–85%); may be missed in anaemia |
| **Stridor** | Harsh, mainly **inspiratory** noise from the larynx/upper trachea | Croup, epiglottitis, foreign body, diphtheria, laryngomalacia; **in a calm child = danger sign** |
| **Wheeze** | Musical, mainly **expiratory** noise from intrathoracic airways | Asthma, bronchiolitis, viral wheeze; **monophonic/unilateral** = foreign body or node compression |
| **Crackles (crepitations)** | Discontinuous, non-musical sounds, mainly inspiratory; **fine** (alveoli: pneumonia, oedema, bronchiolitis) or **coarse** (larger airways: secretions, bronchiectasis) | Parenchymal disease or secretions |
| **Bronchial breathing** | Harsh breath sound with a pause between inspiration and expiration, expiration as long as inspiration | **Consolidation**; above an effusion |
| **Pleural rub** | Creaking sound in both phases | Pleurisy |
| **Clubbing** | Loss of the nail-fold angle, increased curvature, fluctuant nail bed; **Schamroth window** lost | Chronic suppurative lung disease, cyanotic heart disease, HIV/LIP, CF, IBD |
| **Hyperinflation** | Barrel chest, increased AP diameter, liver pushed down, reduced cardiac dullness | Asthma, bronchiolitis |
| **Harrison sulcus** | Horizontal groove along the lower ribs at the diaphragm insertion | Long-standing increased work of breathing (chronic asthma, rickets, heart failure) |

### Key findings and their meaning

| Finding | Meaning |
|---|---|
| RR above cut-off, no indrawing | Pneumonia (WHO), or fever/acidosis/wheeze: classify after excluding wheeze and malaria acidosis |
| Lower chest wall indrawing | Pneumonia (WHO); severe if very marked |
| SpO₂ <90%, grunting, head nodding | Severe pneumonia/hypoxaemia: oxygen now |
| Stridor at rest | Upper-airway obstruction: croup, epiglottitis, foreign body, diphtheria |
| Diffuse polyphonic wheeze | Asthma, viral wheeze, bronchiolitis |
| Unilateral/monophonic wheeze | Foreign body, TB node compression |
| Fine crackles bilaterally in an infant | Bronchiolitis, pneumonia, heart failure |
| Dullness + bronchial breathing | Consolidation |
| Stony dullness + absent breath sounds + trachea shifted away | Effusion/empyema |
| Hyper-resonance + absent sounds + trachea shifted away | Pneumothorax (tension if shocked) |
| Dullness + reduced sounds + trachea pulled towards | Collapse |
| Clubbing | Chronic lung suppuration, HIV/LIP, cyanotic heart disease, CF |
| Red bulging drum | AOM |
| Perforation + discharge ≥14 days | CSOM |
| Exudative tonsils + tender anterior nodes, no cough | GAS tonsillitis |
| Grey adherent membrane, bull neck | Diphtheria |
| Uvula deviated, trismus | Quinsy |
| Firm matted nodes, sinus | TB lymphadenitis |

### WHO/IMCI classification the examination feeds into (2–59 months)

| Findings | Classification |
|---|---|
| Danger sign, SpO₂ <90%/central cyanosis, severe distress, stridor in a calm child | **Severe pneumonia or very severe disease** |
| Fast breathing and/or chest indrawing | **Pneumonia** |
| None of these | **Cough or cold** |

### From signs to localisation

| Pattern | Localisation | Likely diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| Stridor, barking cough, hoarse voice | Larynx/subglottis | Croup |
| Stridor, drooling, toxic, no cough | Supraglottis | Epiglottitis |
| Polyphonic expiratory wheeze, hyperinflation | Small airways (bilateral) | Asthma / viral wheeze |
| Fine crackles + wheeze in an infant | Bronchioles | Bronchiolitis |
| Focal dullness, bronchial breathing, crackles | Parenchyma | Lobar pneumonia |
| Stony dullness, absent sounds, shift away | Pleura | Effusion / empyema |
| Deep fast breathing, clear chest | Outside the lung | Acidosis (malaria, DKA, dehydration) |

### The tympanic membrane

| Finding | Normal | AOM | OME (glue ear) | CSOM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colour | Pearly grey, translucent | **Red/yellow, opaque** | Dull, amber/grey | Varies; discharge |
| Position | Neutral | **Bulging** | **Retracted** (prominent malleus handle) | – |
| Light reflex | Anteroinferior cone | Lost | Distorted | – |
| Landmarks (malleus) | Visible | **Lost** | Prominent | – |
| Other | Mobile (pneumatic) | Reduced mobility; pus if perforated (<14 days) | **Fluid level, bubbles**, reduced mobility | **Perforation** (central or attic) with discharge **≥14 days**; cholesteatoma = white debris, foul smell |

**Other ear findings**: swelling and pain on moving the pinna = otitis externa; mastoid redness, swelling, tenderness with the pinna pushed forward and down = mastoiditis. **Hearing**: Rinne bone > air = conductive loss; Weber lateralises to the conductive-loss ear.

### Tonsils: Brodsky grading

| Grade | Tonsils occupy |
|---|---|
| 0 | Within the tonsillar fossa (or removed) |
| 1+ | <25% of the oropharyngeal width |
| 2+ | 25–50% |
| 3+ | 50–75% |
| 4+ | >75% ("kissing tonsils") |

### Lymph nodes

**Tender anterior cervical nodes** with tonsillitis = GAS; **posterior cervical** with splenomegaly = EBV; **firm, matted, non-tender, ± fluctuant or sinus** = **TB lymphadenitis**; **generalised lymphadenopathy** = HIV, TB, EBV, leukaemia/lymphoma; **hard, fixed, rapidly growing** = malignancy (Burkitt, Hodgkin).

### Bedside tests

| Test | How | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Pulse oximetry** | Correct probe; 30–60 s stable trace | <90% = oxygen |
| **Respiratory timer** | 60 s count | Fast breathing |
| **Peak expiratory flow** | ≥5–6 y; standing; best of 3; compare with height chart | Asthma severity and response |
| **Bronchodilator trial** | Salbutamol via spacer, re-assess after 15–20 min | Reversible wheeze vs pneumonia |
| **Malaria RDT, RBS, HemoCue** | Finger prick | Common co-existing problems |
| **Tuning fork tests** | 512 Hz Rinne/Weber | Conductive vs sensorineural hearing loss |
| **Throat swab / RADT** (if available) | Swab both tonsils and posterior pharynx | GAS |

### Laboratory tests and imaging

| Test | When | Expected / abnormal finding | How it changes management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chest X-ray** | Severe pneumonia, unilateral signs, suspected effusion, pneumothorax, foreign body, TB, not improving | Consolidation, effusion, pneumothorax, hyperinflation, hilar nodes, foreign body | Confirms what the examination suggested; drains, bronchoscopy, TB treatment. HC IV (some), hospitals |
| **Chest ultrasound** | Dullness: effusion vs consolidation | Fluid, septations | Tap/drain site. RRH/NRH |
| **Lateral neck X-ray** (stable child only) | Retropharyngeal abscess, radiopaque foreign body | Prevertebral widening, foreign body | ENT/surgery |
| **FBC** | Severe infection, suspected malignancy (nodes) | Neutrophilia, blasts, atypical lymphocytes | Antibiotics, oncology referral |
| **Ear swab** | CSOM not drying | Pseudomonas, Staph | Targeted drops |
| **FNA of node** | Persistent node >2 cm, matted, sinus | Caseation, AFB, Xpert; lymphoma cells | TB treatment vs oncology |
| **Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra** | Chronic cough, TB contact, nodes | MTB detected | TB treatment |
| **Audiometry/tympanometry** | Hearing concern, glue ear, CSOM | Conductive loss; flat tympanogram | ENT (Mulago, Mengo ENT clinics) |
| **HIV test** | Clubbing, thrush, generalised nodes, CSOM, recurrent pneumonia | Positive | ART, cotrimoxazole |

### Worked examples

> [!EXAM]
> **Worked example 1: interpreting a set of chest signs.** A 6-year-old: RR 34, SpO₂ 92%, trachea deviated to the left, reduced expansion on the right, **stony dull** percussion right base, **absent breath sounds** and absent vocal resonance there, bronchial breathing just above the dull area. **Interpretation**: a **right pleural effusion** (mediastinum pushed away; bronchial breathing above the fluid from compressed lung). With 3 weeks of fever and a TB contact → TB effusion vs empyema → CXR/ultrasound and diagnostic tap.

> [!EXAM]
> **Worked example 2: reading two eardrums.** A 15-month-old with fever and irritability. Right drum: **red, bulging, no light reflex, malleus not seen** → **acute otitis media**. Left drum: pearly grey, light reflex present → normal. Plan: amoxicillin 40 mg/kg twice daily for 5 days, analgesia, review in 5 days. A 5-year-old with 2 months of painless discharge: canal full of pus; after dry mopping, a **central perforation** is seen → **CSOM**: wicking + ciprofloxacin ear drops for 2 weeks, HIV test, hearing assessment.

## 7. Clinical Acumen

### First 5 minutes: is it safe to examine fully, and in what order?

1. **ETAT triage at the door**: Airway/Breathing (obstructed breathing, central cyanosis, severe respiratory distress), Circulation (cold hands + CRT >3 s + weak fast pulse), Coma/Convulsions, Dehydration (severe). If any emergency sign → **resuscitate first** (oxygen, airway positioning, IV access, glucose), examine later.
2. **Priority signs** (3TPR-MOB): tiny baby (<2 months), temperature (very high), trauma, pallor, poisoning, pain (severe), respiratory distress, restless/irritable/lethargic, referral, malnutrition (visible severe wasting), oedema of both feet, burns → examine immediately.
3. **Airway caution**: stridor/drooling → no throat exam, no lying down, call help.
4. **SpO₂ within the first minutes** in any child with respiratory symptoms.
5. **Infection control**: if TB or pertussis is suspected in an adolescent/adult carer, masks and ventilation; hand hygiene before and after.

### Focused history: why each question matters before you examine

- **Age and weight** → normal RR/HR, fast-breathing cut-off, drug doses, likely diagnoses (bronchiolitis <2 y; croup 6 m–3 y; GAS ≥3 y).
- **Main symptom and duration** → acute vs chronic pathway.
- **Noisy breathing: when, what sound?** Inspiratory (stridor) vs expiratory (wheeze); sudden (foreign body) vs gradual.
- **Choking episode** → foreign body.
- **Danger signs** (drink, vomit, convulsions, lethargy) → severe disease regardless of your chest findings.
- **Fever pattern and duration** → bacterial vs viral; >2 weeks → TB.
- **Ear discharge duration** (<14 vs ≥14 days) → acute vs chronic; hearing and speech → hearing loss.
- **Drooling, voice change** → epiglottitis, quinsy (don't examine the throat).
- **HIV status, TB contact, immunisations (PCV, Hib, DTP, MR), nutrition** → change the meaning of your findings (clubbing, nodes, thrush).

### Red flags: stop the routine examination and act

> [!REDFLAG]
> Stop the routine examination and act if you find: **stridor at rest, drooling or tripod posture**; **SpO₂ <90% or central cyanosis**; **grunting, head nodding, severe indrawing, apnoea**; **silent chest** in a wheezy child; **tracheal deviation with shock** (tension pneumothorax); **lethargy or inability to drink**; **mastoid swelling, neck stiffness or facial palsy** with an ear infection; **grey membrane** in the throat.

**Emergency flow if you find a dangerous sign during bedside teaching**

1. **Stop teaching**; tell the tutor/nurse clearly: "This child has SpO₂ 84% / stridor at rest / a silent chest."
2. **Call for help** and move the child to the resuscitation area if needed.
3. **ABC**: position, oxygen, suction if needed (not in epiglottitis), IV access, glucose.
4. **Specific first treatment** (section 8).
5. **Document** time, findings and actions; hand over to the team.

### Thinking like a clinician

- **Count, don't estimate**: the most common OSCE and ward error is a guessed RR.
- **Indrawing must be seen in a calm child**: a crying child "indraws" normally.
- **Wheeze + fast breathing**: give a bronchodilator and re-count before calling it pneumonia (WHO/IMCI).
- **Deep, fast breathing with a clear chest** = **acidosis** (severe malaria, DKA, dehydration, salicylates), not pneumonia.
- **Crackles + hepatomegaly + murmur** = heart failure (VSD, RHD); don't just give antibiotics.
- **Silence can be dangerous**: a "quiet chest" in asthma or "softening stridor" in croup may mean exhaustion.
- **Unilateral signs in a toddler without fever** = foreign body until proven otherwise.
- **Fever without a source**: always look in the ears and throat, and do a malaria test.
- **Clubbing is never normal**: it changes the differential to chronic disease (bronchiectasis, HIV, TB sequelae, heart disease).
- **Nodes**: a single firm matted cervical node in a child with a TB contact is TB until an FNA says otherwise.

### Common student mistakes

Guessing the RR or counting for 15 s and multiplying; calling intercostal recession in a crying child "chest indrawing"; forgetting the axillae; calling transmitted upper-airway sounds crackles; pulling the pinna up in an infant; reaching for a tongue depressor in a drooling child; diagnosing pneumonia in a wheezy or acidotic child without a bronchodilator trial or malaria test; not mentioning SpO₂.

## 8. Complications and Troubleshooting

### Complications of the examination and how to avoid them

| Complication | Prevention |
|---|---|
| **Complete airway obstruction** from throat examination or lying down in epiglottitis/severe croup | Never examine the throat with stridor/drooling; keep upright; call airway help |
| **Inaccurate findings** (RR, SpO₂, indrawing) from an upset child | Examine calm child first, opportunistically, on the parent's lap |
| **Ear canal trauma, bleeding, TM perforation** | Largest speculum that fits, brace hand, gentle traction, still child |
| **Pushing a foreign body deeper** | Look first; refer if not easily removable |
| **Gagging, vomiting, aspiration** | Tongue depressor on the anterior tongue, briefly |
| **Cross-infection** (otoscope specula, stethoscope, hands) | Hand hygiene; clean equipment between children |
| **Hypothermia** in neonates/small infants during undressing | Examine under a warmer/blanket, expose briefly |
| **Distress and loss of trust** | Explain, involve parent, distraction, praise |

### Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| Child crying, RR and indrawing unreadable | Settle on the parent's lap, feed or distract, recount; do not classify a crying child |
| SpO₂ reading unstable | Warm the limb, keep it still, change probe size or site, wait for a steady pleth trace |
| Borderline SpO₂ 90–93% with distress | Treat as possible hypoxaemia (oximeters over-read in dark skin), repeat, watch the child |
| Coarse sounds everywhere in an infant | Listen over the neck, clear the nose or let the child cough, then re-listen |
| Wax hides the drum | Softening drops; review; no cotton buds |
| Canal full of pus | Dry mop, then look for a perforation |

### Acting on abnormal findings at the bedside (management)

The examination itself is not a treatment, but **what you do with each finding** is the reason you examine.

| Finding | Immediate action |
|---|---|
| **SpO₂ <90%** or central cyanosis | **Oxygen** by nasal prongs (0.5–1 L/min neonates; 1–2 L/min infants; 2–4 L/min older children), target ≥90% (≥94% if emergency signs); inform senior; classify as severe |
| **Fast breathing ± indrawing, no danger signs** | Classify **pneumonia** → oral amoxicillin 40 mg/kg twice daily × 5 days (see LRTI topic) |
| **Danger sign / severe distress** | **Severe pneumonia** → admit, oxygen, ampicillin 50 mg/kg 6-hourly + gentamicin 7.5 mg/kg daily |
| **Wheeze with fast breathing** | Salbutamol via spacer (2–4 puffs) and re-count in 15–20 min |
| **Stridor at rest** | Keep calm, oxygen without distress, dexamethasone 0.15–0.6 mg/kg (croup), nebulised adrenaline if severe; epiglottitis → call anaesthetist/ENT, no throat exam |
| **Silent chest / exhaustion in asthma** | Life-threatening asthma: back-to-back salbutamol + ipratropium, IV magnesium, senior help |
| **Tension pneumothorax** (shock + tracheal deviation + hyper-resonance) | **Needle decompression** (2nd intercostal space, mid-clavicular line, or 4th–5th ICS anterior axillary line), then chest drain |
| **Stony dullness** | CXR/ultrasound, diagnostic tap, chest drain if pus |
| **AOM** | Amoxicillin 40 mg/kg twice daily × 5 days (WHO/IMCI), paracetamol 15 mg/kg |
| **CSOM** | Teach wicking, ciprofloxacin drops 2 weeks, HIV test, hearing check |
| **Mastoid swelling** | Admit; ceftriaxone 50–80 mg/kg + metronidazole; urgent ENT |
| **Exudative tonsillitis (McIsaac 4–5)** | Penicillin V 10–20 mg/kg 6-hourly × 10 days or benzathine penicillin once |
| **Grey membrane** | Isolate, diphtheria antitoxin, penicillin/erythromycin, airway watch |
| **Foreign body in ear/nose** | Don't push it in; remove only if easily grasped with proper instruments (crocodile forceps, hook) and a still child; otherwise ENT. **Button batteries** in the nose/ear = urgent removal |
| **Wax impaction** | Softening drops (olive oil/sodium bicarbonate) for a few days; no cotton buds |
| **Matted cervical nodes** | TB workup (Xpert on FNA, CXR, HIV) |
| **Clubbing** | Look for chronic lung disease, HIV, heart disease, CF; CXR, HIV test, echo |

### Aftercare following examination

- Re-dress and comfort the child; thank the carer and explain findings in plain language.
- **Clean equipment**: otoscope specula (disposable or alcohol-cleaned), stethoscope diaphragm (alcohol wipe) between patients; **hand hygiene** (WHO 5 moments).
- **Document** all findings with times, RR counts and SpO₂ (in air/O₂), and the classification.
- **Plan re-examination**: severe pneumonia RR and SpO₂ at least 3-hourly; asthma every 20–30 min in the first hour; croup after steroid/adrenaline.

## 9. Documentation and Presenting Findings

### What to document

| Document these positives | And these negatives |
|---|---|
| RR (60 s) vs cut-off; SpO₂ (air/O₂) | "No danger signs; no central cyanosis" |
| Indrawing, flaring, grunting, head nodding | "No stridor in a calm child" |
| Focal chest signs (side, zone) | "Air entry equal; no wheeze" |
| Drum appearance each side | "No mastoid swelling" |
| Tonsil grade, exudate, nodes | "No membrane; uvula central; no drooling" |
| Clubbing, growth Z-scores | "No hepatosplenomegaly" |

### Presenting your examination findings

> "On general examination, Amina is a 2-year-old girl, alert, sitting on her mother's lap, not cyanosed or pale, weighing 11 kg (WAZ −0.8). Her respiratory rate is 48 per minute counted over one minute, which is fast for her age; SpO₂ is 93% in room air; heart rate 136; temperature 38.6 °C. She has nasal flaring and lower chest wall indrawing but no grunting or head nodding. The trachea is central. Chest expansion appears reduced on the right. Percussion is dull at the right lower zone posteriorly, with bronchial breathing and fine crackles there; the left side is clear; no wheeze. Ears: both drums pearly grey with a normal light reflex. Throat: mildly red, tonsils grade 1 without exudate. Small, soft, mobile cervical nodes under 1 cm. No clubbing, no hepatosplenomegaly. **In summary**, she has fast breathing, chest indrawing and signs of right lower lobe consolidation without danger signs or hypoxaemia: **pneumonia (WHO classification)**, right lower lobe."

### Model case presentation (full)

> "Amina is a 2-year-old girl from Kawempe weighing 11 kg (WAZ −0.8, WHZ −0.5), fully immunised per UNEPI including PCV ×3 and MR1, HIV-negative, breastfed to 18 months, now on a family diet, with 3 days of fever and cough. On examination she is alert on her mother's lap, not cyanosed, RR 48 counted over a minute (fast for her age; cut-off 40), SpO₂ 93% in air, HR 136, T 38.6 °C, with nasal flaring and lower chest wall indrawing, no grunting or head nodding. Trachea central. Dull percussion, bronchial breathing and fine crackles at the right base posteriorly; left clear; no wheeze. Ears and throat normal; small soft cervical nodes. No clubbing, no hepatosplenomegaly. Malaria RDT negative. Assessment: **pneumonia (WHO), right lower lobe, without danger signs or hypoxaemia**. Plan: oral amoxicillin 440 mg (40 mg/kg) twice daily for 5 days, paracetamol, home care advice, review in 2–3 days or earlier if danger signs."

### Short presentations for common conditions

- **Asthma**: "RR 38, HR 138, SpO₂ 89% in air, speaks in words, intercostal recession, widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze, equal air entry, PEF 40%: acute severe asthma."
- **Bronchiolitis**: "4-month-old, RR 62, SpO₂ 89%, nasal flaring, subcostal recession, bilateral fine inspiratory crackles and expiratory wheeze, hyperinflated chest, liver edge 3 cm below the costal margin (pushed down): severe bronchiolitis."
- **Croup**: "Barking cough, hoarse cry, inspiratory stridor at rest, moderate suprasternal and subcostal recession, alert, SpO₂ 95%, no drooling: moderate croup; throat not examined."

## 11. Key Points

### High-yield points

- Look before you touch, count before you listen, and leave the throat until last.
- Count the RR for a full 60 seconds in a calm child; fast breathing is 60–50–40 (>30 from 5 years).
- Lower chest wall indrawing = lower chest wall in on inspiration, all the time, in a calm child.
- SpO₂ <90% = oxygen; oximeters may over-read in dark skin.
- Stridor is inspiratory and upper airway; wheeze is expiratory and intrathoracic; stridor at rest is a danger sign.
- Never examine the throat with stridor, drooling, tripod posture or a toxic look.
- Always listen in the axillae.
- Pinna down and back in infants, up and back in older children.
- AOM: red bulging drum; OME: dull retracted drum with fluid; CSOM: perforation with discharge ≥14 days.
- Clubbing is never normal, and asthma does not cause it.
- Finish with a WHO/IMCI classification.

### Exam traps

- **Guessing the RR** or counting for 15 s and multiplying: inaccurate; count 60 s.
- **Calling intercostal recession in a crying child "chest indrawing"**: must be lower chest wall, all the time, calm child.
- **Examining the throat in a drooling child**: dangerous.
- **Pulling the pinna up in an infant**: down and back for infants.
- **Calling transmitted upper-airway sounds "crackles"**: listen over the neck and after a cough.
- **Forgetting the axillae**: the right middle lobe and lingula are heard there.
- **Saying "asthma causes clubbing"**: it doesn't.
- **Diagnosing pneumonia from fast breathing in a wheezy or acidotic child** without a bronchodilator trial or malaria test.

### Questions seniors ask

**Viva questions**

1. *How do you count the respiratory rate in an infant?* Calm child, chest/abdomen exposed, watch the abdomen, count a full 60 s with a timer, repeat if borderline.
2. *Define lower chest wall indrawing.* The lower chest wall goes in when the child breathes in, present all the time in a calm child.
3. *How do you differentiate stridor from wheeze?* Stridor is inspiratory, from the extrathoracic airway, loudest over the neck; wheeze is expiratory, musical, from intrathoracic airways, heard over the chest.
4. *How do you straighten the ear canal in an infant vs an older child?* Pull the pinna down and back in infants; up and back in older children.
5. *What does a normal eardrum look like?* Pearly grey, translucent, with a cone of light anteroinferiorly and the handle of the malleus visible.
6. *When must you not examine the throat?* Stridor, drooling, tripod posture, muffled voice or toxic look: suspected epiglottitis.
7. *Causes of clubbing in a Ugandan child?* Bronchiectasis (post-measles/pertussis/TB), HIV lung disease (LIP), cyanotic congenital heart disease, empyema/lung abscess, CF, IBD, liver cirrhosis, infective endocarditis.

**On the ward round**

- **What is the respiratory rate and is it fast?** State the number counted over 60 s and the WHO cut-off for age ("54/min, fast for a 9-month-old: cut-off 50").
- **Show me chest indrawing.** Point to the lower chest wall moving in on inspiration in a calm child, and contrast with intercostal recession.
- **Why do you listen in the axillae?** The right middle lobe and lingula project there; consolidation can be missed from front and back alone.
- **What would make you stop and call for help?** Stridor at rest with drooling, SpO₂ <90%, grunting/head nodding, silent chest, tracheal shift with shock, lethargy.
- **What do you see in this eardrum?** Describe colour, translucency, position (bulging/retracted), light reflex, malleus, perforation/discharge, then name it (normal, AOM, OME, CSOM).
- **How would you examine the throat of a 2-year-old?** On the parent's lap, arms and head held, torch, ask to open wide, tongue depressor briefly on the anterior tongue, look at tonsils, palate, uvula, pharynx, buccal mucosa; not at all if stridor/drooling.
- **How do you tell an effusion from consolidation at the bedside?** Both are dull; effusion is stony dull with absent breath sounds and vocal resonance and may push the trachea away; consolidation has bronchial breathing, increased vocal resonance and crackles.

### Mnemonics

- **Order: "Look, Count, Measure, Touch, Listen, then Ears-Nose-Throat."**
- **WHO fast breathing "60–50–40"** (plus >30 at ≥5 years).
- **Severe distress "GHINC"**: **G**runting, **H**ead nodding, **I**ndrawing (severe), **N**asal flaring, **C**yanosis.
- **Pinna: "babies Down, big kids Up"** (both backwards).
- **Effusion vs collapse "push vs pull"**: effusion and pneumothorax **push** the trachea away; collapse **pulls** it towards.
- **Normal drum "Pearly, Light, Handle"**: pearly grey, light reflex, handle of malleus.
- **Node description "SCTMF"**: **S**ize/site, **C**onsistency, **T**enderness, **M**obility/matting, **F**luctuation (and skin).

### OSCE tips

- **Say what you are doing and why** ("I am counting for a full minute because the child is calm now").
- **State normal values out loud** for the child's age.
- **Always mention SpO₂**, even if the examiner did not provide an oximeter ("I would like to measure SpO₂").
- **Axillae**: examiners watch whether you listen there.
- **ENT last**, and **voice the epiglottitis rule** before touching a tongue depressor.
- **Finish with a classification** (WHO/IMCI) and two differentials.
